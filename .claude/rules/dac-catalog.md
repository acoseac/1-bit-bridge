# The DAC catalog: the player's DAC profiles and their iOS projection (B730 B1)

`internal/player/dacprofile` is the DAC catalog: the bridge player's DAC profile database (B728, whose rules stay in `player.md`) and, since B730 B1 (2026-11-17, schema 2), the one source of the iOS app's DAC registry, which `scripts/dac-profiles/ios-export` writes as `DACCatalog.ios.json` for the app to bundle and pin. Each entry is one line ending `→ History-rules-dac-catalog.md`, which is `ops/History-rules-dac-catalog.md`, where it sits with its reasoning; the record is in `ops/engineering-log.md` under 2026-11-17.

## The catalog and its iOS projection (B730 B1)

- **A row with no USB match is iOS's alone: it carries `ios`, makes no claim only a match reaches (no identity line, native claim, timing, analogue or raw-maximum control), and matches no unit; a row gains a match only from a capture or a kernel quirk, which its identity line names.** **Don't give an iOS-only row a guessed ID or a match taken from a name, let `Match` read `ios` or compare a name, or put a claim only a match reaches on a row without one.** `TestAnIOSOnlyRowMatchesNoUnit`, `TestTheQX13CarriesItsHiddenModeCaveat`, `TestEveryClaimNamesItsProvenance` and `TestTheSchemaRefusesWhatItDoesNotDescribe` pin it. → History-rules-dac-catalog.md
- **DoP evidence is per rate: `heardBitRates` are the rates a listening heard, each heard itself (a heard rate says nothing of the rates below it, and the player reads it so), `refusedBitRates` the rates that failed (`refused`, `silence at`, `noise at`, `no frames at`), and the statement one of three (`stated DoP to`, `stated DSD to`, `stated no DSD`), each credited by its dop clauses both ways; in a row with `ios` every marker clause opens with its source's head, a `submission`'s and a `field log`'s with its date, and one clause credits each rate and the statement.** **Don't read a heard rate as a maximum, file a DSD figure that names no transport as `statedMaxBitRate` (it is `statedWithoutDoP`, which the player never reads), credit a lock, a negotiation or a sibling's result as heard, put a family row's siblings' results in its own rates, name a person in a source, or ask a date of the four head kinds that carry none (`maker`, `pcm ceiling`, `chip specification`, `retailer`) or of a clause in a row without `ios`.** `TestEachDoPRateIsCreditedRateByRate`, `TestAFailureBelowAHeardRateIsKept`, `TestAStatementIsOneOfThree`, `TestAnIOSRowsClausesNameTheirSource`, `TestTheLegacyCeilingAgreesWithTheEvidence`, `TestNoEmbeddedDoPLineReadsAsAHearing`, `TestTheCapturedModelsAreSeededAsTheirEvidenceSays`, `TestAHeardRateAdmitsOnlyItself` and `TestAStatementWithoutDoPAdmitsNoDoP` pin it. → History-rules-dac-catalog.md
- **The export writes the rows with `ios` by `ios.order`, their legacy patterns and ceiling verbatim until iOS PR 3 retires them, deterministically, as its golden `DACCatalog.ios.json`, whose SHA-256 iOS PR 2 pins; it is held to the iOS registry's baseline the dispatch gave (`testdata/ios-registry-3145606a.json`, pinned by its SHA-256), never to itself.** **Don't hand-edit the golden or the baseline, fix a parity failure anywhere but the catalog, order the rows otherwise, iterate a map unsorted, or export a field only the bridge reads.** `TestTheExportIsItsGolden`, `TestTheExportIsDeterministic`, `TestAnUnrecordedRowExportsNoEvidence` and `TestTheExportMatchesTheIOSBaseline` pin it. → History-rules-dac-catalog.md
- **The name keys are iOS PR 1's, printable ASCII, compared with A to Z folded: tokens of four characters or more; no token and generic name one inside the other; across two rows only, no token and the other's token or port name one inside the other; port names unique and none a generic name; no excluded token inside its own row's token; orders 1 to N, each once; and no known sibling (`iosKnownSiblings`) answered with a model row by the strict matcher, by a port name or by a token.** **Don't seed a token from a legacy pattern (`KA3`, `TOPPING E30`), hold a row's own token against its own port name, refuse an excluded token that holds its row's own token (`JM98MAX 2`), let a name outside printable ASCII in, or let a known sibling reach a model row, by a port name, which no excluded token rules out, or by a token.** `TestTheIOSNameKeysLint`, `TestNoKnownSiblingReachesAModelRow`, `TestTheExportMatchesTheIOSBaseline` and `TestTheSchemaRefusesWhatItDoesNotDescribe` pin it. → History-rules-dac-catalog.md
