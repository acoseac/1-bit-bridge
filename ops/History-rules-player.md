## Player DSD containers (B470 PR 1b)

- **Canonical native DSD is stereo, channel-separated bytes, bit 7 earliest** (`format.NativeDSD`). DSF with 1 bit per sample is LSB-first and each byte is bit-reversed; DSF with 8 is MSB-first and passes unchanged; uncompressed DFF passes unchanged. `Frames` is one-bit samples per channel and `BytesPerChannel` rounds it up: when the DSF sample count is not a multiple of eight, the last byte's trailing bits are the file's padding, passed as they are, and the transport accounts for them. `BytesPerChannel` divides before it rounds, because `(Frames+7)/8` wraps negative for a `Frames` within 7 of `math.MaxInt64`, which a DSF header can declare beside a data chunk declared large enough to pass open (round 2). It is 0 for a `Frames` of 0 or less and at most 2^60, and the DSF decoder's end is that same value, so a seek to `BytesPerChannel` is always the decoder's end. **Don't convert DSD to PCM in a demuxer or anywhere but `dsdconv`, don't reverse a byte the container declared MSB-first, and don't round a DSD count up by adding before dividing.** `TestNativeDSDBytesPerChannel` and `TestDSFHugeSampleCountKeepsItsByteArithmetic` pin the count.

- **Accepted:** DSF version 1, format id 0 (DSD raw), channel type 2 with 2 channels, block size 4096, 1 or 8 bits per sample; DFF `FRM8`/`DSD ` whose first chunk is `FVER`, four bytes, major version 1 (DSDIFF 1.5 §3 and §3.1; the minor version is not checked), then `PROP`/`SND ` holding `FS  `, `CHNL` (`SLFT`,`SRGT` in either order: the file's order is mapped back to left, right) and `CMPR` `DSD `. Both at 2,822,400 Hz × 1, 2, 4 or 8 (DSD64..DSD512); the 48 kHz family and DSD1024 are refused. A DFF without `FVER`, with another chunk first, with a second `FVER` before the sound data, with an `FVER` that is not four bytes, or of another major version is `unsupported_layout`, and the refusal names `FVER`; a file cut inside the version is `truncated_or_corrupt`. A second `FVER` after the sound data is not refused: the walk returns at the sound chunk and never reads past it. A channel count other than 2 is `unsupported_channel_count`; DST (in `CMPR` or as a `DST ` sound chunk) and any other compression is `unsupported_compression`, never decoded. **Don't decode DST, don't take the first two channels of a multichannel file, and don't skip `FVER` as an unknown chunk.** `TestDFFFormatVersionRequired` pins `FVER`, and `TestDFFChunkBombStopsAtTheCap` that the chunk-cap fixture, which needs an `FVER` to reach the cap, is refused by the cap.

- **PR 1a's invariants hold for DSD.** Every file size is compared as `uint64` against what is addressable before any `int64` conversion, and every bound subtracts from what remains instead of adding to a position: the DFF walk asks `frmEnd-pos < 12` before it reads a chunk header and bounds each chunk with `dffChunkEnd`, because near `math.MaxInt64` a sum wraps negative and passes the check meant to stop it (round 3: crafted sizes walked a DFF to `MaxInt64-1`, where `pos+12` wrapped and the walk read a header past its form). The DSF and DFF chunk walks and PROP's sub-chunks are capped at 256, PROP's body at 64 KiB; the DFF sound chunk and every PROP sub-chunk must sit inside their parent. **Don't bound a size by adding it to a position.** `TestDFFWalkReadsNothingPastItsForm`, which records every read request because a read past the data returns EOF and looks like truncation, and `TestDFFChunkEndBoundsBySubtraction` pin it. DFF reads go through `linearRegion`, the same bounded read WAV and AIFF use (`pcmRegion` embeds it): capped at the sound data, `ctx` checked after each read, the reader seeked back on a cancel or an I/O error. DSF reads one block group's span at a time and seeks to its absolute offset before each read, so a cancel or an I/O error leaves nothing to undo; a failed seek sticks until a successful `Seek`. A file cut short returns the whole bytes before the cut (every channel the same count) with a `truncated_or_corrupt` refusal, `n > 0` with the error as the `Decoder` contract allows. **Don't add a second linear reader beside `linearRegion`.**

- **DSD goldens are an independently specified pattern**, not a decoder round trip. `scripts/player-dsd-goldens/generate.py` writes the DSF and DFF fixtures from the specifications around `pattern.bin`: no byte is a bit palindrome, left and right differ at every index, and `0x00` never occurs, so a missed reversal, a channel swap or padding read as audio each change bytes. The fixtures are marked `binary` in `.gitattributes` because `pattern.bin` has no NUL. **The DFF goldens give both chunk walks an odd chunk before the sound data**: every DFF golden has a 7-byte `MANF` before `PROP` for the form's walk, since §3.6 pads a `COMT` comment inside its body, so a `COMT` is always even; and every DFF golden but `dst.dff`, a refusal fixture whose `CMPR` ("DST Encoded") is 16 bytes, has the 19-byte `CMPR` of §3.2.3 for `PROP`'s walk. The two accepted DFF goldens, `dsd64.dff` and `dsd256_right_first.dff`, have both. Until round 2 the generator padded `CMPR` inside its size and `COMT` outside it, every `PROP` sub-chunk was even, and a `PROP` walk without its pad skip passed every test. **Don't make every chunk of a DFF golden even.** The demuxer skips `COMT` and `MANF` by their sizes, so no decoding test sees their layout: `TestDFFGoldensCOMTFollowsSection36` and `TestDFFGoldensGiveTheWalksAnOddChunk` read the committed bytes instead (round 3). To check a generator change without touching the committed fixtures, write into a scratch directory with `generate.py --out DIR` and run `go test ./internal/player/decode -args -dsd-goldens=DIR`. `TestDSDGoldensMatchPattern`, `TestDSDGoldensExactSeek`, `TestDSDGoldensEOFAtSampleCount`, `TestDSDGoldensRefused`, `TestDSDTruncatedMidBlock`, `TestDSDAfterID3Prefix`, `TestDSDAbsurdSizesRefused`, `TestDSFMetadataPointerIgnored`, `TestDSDCancelThenRetryNoGap`, `TestDSDIOErrorThenRetryNoGap`, `TestDSDReadBufferShape`, `FuzzDSFOpen` and `FuzzDFFOpen` pin it.

- **The sample count ends DSF audio, never the data size.** The final block group is zero-padded to 4096 per channel; the data chunk must hold every block group the sample count needs, or the file is `truncated_or_corrupt` at open. The `DSD ` chunk's metadata pointer and total size are never used, so ID3 metadata is never read as audio. Before the sound data, DFF chunks other than `FVER` and `PROP` (`COMT`, `MANF`, and `DIIN` or `ID3 ` where a writer puts them there) are skipped by their sizes plus the pad byte after an odd body (§2.3), in the form's walk and in `PROP`'s; `PROP` must precede the sound data. The walk validates chunk headers through the form end, seeking over audio and metadata bodies; a second PROP after sound, trailing property bytes, missing padding or bytes outside the form refuse playback. `CMPR` is read only for its four-byte compressionType, so a `CMPR` whose size counts the name's pad (20 bytes for "not compressed", the form `internal/dsdtone` writes) opens like §3.2.3's 19-byte one (`TestDFFCMPRWithItsNamePadInsideStillOpens`). **Don't follow the DSF metadata pointer.**

## Player streaming PCM sink (B470 PR 2b)

- **`bridge output pcm-test` drives the sink for the PR 2b gate.** `--sink` plays the tone through it, `--tracks N` split mid-cycle into tracks of one transport; `--file` decodes a file and plays it bit-exact, at the file's own level, which the bridge never lowers, so it needs `--ack-full-level`, and a second `--file` plays back to back, gapless when the transport matches and through a drain and a new sink, an honest gap, when it does not. `--pause-at` and `--resume-after` pause and resume on a timer, `--keys` on p and q from standard input; `--pause auto|pause|zeros`, `--release-after`, and `--recover`. - **One writer goroutine owns the Device, fed through a store preallocated at open.** `alsapcm.Sink` copies the producer's frames into a ring of `DefaultLookahead` (2 s) ahead of the hardware buffer plus the hardware buffer's frames kept behind the write point, capped at `MaxRingBytes` (32 MiB); `Write` allocates nothing, takes one producer at a time, and waits while the store is full, until its context ends (the frames taken counted). A frame counts once it is published, and the sink's state is asked again under the lock that publishes it, so a Drain, an xrun or an end that comes while `Write` copies takes none of the frames being copied (#1303's review: a Drain that started and finished during the copy was answered while the Write returned the frames as taken). The writer moves frames into the device a period at a time with `Device.Write`, which continues partial writes, and measures after each. `Pause`, `Resume`, `Recover`, `Drain` and `Drop` are commands to it from any goroutine: a command ends whatever wait the writer is in by cancelling that wait's context, and a wait started with a command pending ends at once. `Changed` tells a caller of a state change without polling. **Don't touch the Device from another goroutine, don't allocate on the producer's path, don't size the store past MaxRingBytes, and don't publish frames without asking the sink's state under the same lock.** `TestSinkGivesTheKernelEveryFrameOnceInOrder`, `TestSinkWriteAllocatesNothing`, `TestSinkStoreIsTwoSecondsAheadAndCapped` and `TestSinkTakesNoFramesADrainHasFinished` pin it.

A `Write` that submits frames while the writer watches cancels the wait. What the wait finds is settled as a paused writer's wait is (`settle`): an xrun is a gap found by `wait`, and a suspend or a disconnect ends the sink (#1303's review: a writer that waited on its kick channel alone missed all three until the producer's next frames or a command). **Don't wait for frames without watching the stream.** `TestSinkWatchesTheStreamWhileTheProducerIsLate` pins it.

- **PAUSE's int is the ioctl's argument itself.** SNDRV_PCM_IOCTL_PAUSE is `_IOW('A', 0x45, int)`, and the kernel reads `(unsigned long)arg`, never through a pointer (alsa-lib passes it the same way), so it goes through `sys.ioctlValue`, which keeps the value out of an `unsafe.Pointer`. It is in the pinned UAPI, `probe.c` and the ABI facts; ENOSYS is typed `parameters_not_supported`. **Don't pass PAUSE a pointer, and don't put an integer in an unsafe.Pointer.** `TestPauseUsesTheKernelsPAUSE`, `TestPauseIsRefusedByHardwareThatCannot`, `TestIoctlNumbersEncodeTheirStructures` and `TestProbeAndGoNameTheSameFacts` pin it.

- **A panic on the sink's writer ends the sink typed (`ReasonPanicked`), its device closed, and answers whatever waited on the writer; a node a panic comes to between its open and a sink taking it is closed** (B523). The writer's deferred `recovered` lets go of the sink's lock when a panic left the writer holding it (every hold the writer's goroutine takes goes through `lockW` and `unlockW`, which keep `wHeld`, the writer's alone; holds on other goroutines, a Drain's `afterFunc` callback among them, take `s.mu` as before), logs the panic once through `SinkConfig.Log` (the backend passes its own; nil logs as the player's component) with where, the endpoint and the stack, and the value only when it is the runtime's own error, closes the device (`closeAfterPanic`: a second panic, from that close, is let go of and logged, the device left as it is, and the sink still ends), and ends the sink `SinkEnded` with an `*Error` coded `panicked` that names no value. A node is closed by a deferred close wherever it is not handed on: `openSink` (a panic in the check after open, `SinkConfig.Admit`, which goes on to the caller), `Device`'s `open` (a panic in the kernel's setup) and the writer's `reopen` (a panic in the check after a reopen, which then ends the sink as above); these replaced the explicit closes on each refusal. ## Player ALSA PCM backend (B470 PR 2a)

- **The kernel interface is pinned and checked, never vendored.** `internal/alsapcm/uapi.go` holds the parts of `include/uapi/sound/asound.h` the backend uses, as a 64-bit little-endian process sees them (linux/amd64 and linux/arm64), from the tag `uapiKernel` names (`v6.12`). `scripts/alsa-abi/check.sh` fetches the header at that tag, compiles `probe.c` against it with the machine's own compiler, runs it, and runs `TestUAPIMatchesTheKernelHeader` on its output: every size, offset, field size, interval bit, constant and ioctl number. The `alsa-abi` job in `gate.yml` runs it natively on both architectures (the arm64 leg on GitHub's arm64 runner) and fails unless that test ran and passed, because without `-abi-probe` it skips. On every OS, `TestProbeAndGoNameTheSameFacts` holds the probe's macro calls and the Go side to the same facts and `TestIoctlNumbersEncodeTheirStructures` each request number to its structure's size; a reorder of two same-size fields is caught only by the `alsa-abi` job. **Don't vendor asound.h, don't change an ioctl number or a structure without the other, and don't add a definition the probe does not print.** Moving the pin is in `scripts/alsa-abi/README.md`.

- **An endpoint is two numbers.** `alsapcm.Endpoint{Card, Device}` names `/dev/snd/pcmC<card>D<device>p` and nothing else: there is no string form, so `default`, `hw:`, `plughw:`, dmix, softvol, an alias or a path cannot reach `Open`, and no ALSA configuration is read. The node is looked at with `Lstat` before it is opened (`O_NOFOLLOW`, non-blocking) and with `Fstat` after, and must be an ALSA character device (major 116); INFO must then say it is that card, that device, playback. **Don't add a way to name an endpoint by string, and don't open anything but the node an Endpoint names.** `TestOpenOpensOnlyTheEndpointsPlaybackNode`, `TestOpenChecksTheNodeIsTheEndpointAskedFor`, `TestLinuxOpenRefusesANodeThatIsNotALSA` and `TestOutputPCMTestTakesAnEndpointOnlyByNumber` pin it.

- **Rate, channels and format are exact; the buffer geometry may vary.** The request (RW_INTERLEAVED, the format, STD, two channels, the rate) is refined exactly; a refusal is `parameters_not_supported`, naming the formats, channels and rates the hardware offers. After HW_PARAMS the committed access, format, channels and rate are read back: each must be the one asked for, a committed interval is read only as a closed single value (the kernel's own runtime takes its minimum), and `rate_num` must be the rate times `rate_den`. Anything else is `parameters_mismatch`, and the open frees the parameters and closes the node: nothing is taken in its place. The buffer is aimed at `DefaultBufferTime` (200 ms) in four periods, each tried exactly and then as a range, and `Params` reports what was granted. **Don't accept a committed value other than the one asked for, and don't read a committed interval any other way.** `TestOpenCommitsExactlyWhatWasAsked`, `TestOpenRefusesWhatTheHardwareDoesNotTake`, `TestOpenRefusesAReadBackThatIsNotWhatWasAsked`, `TestOpenTakesTheGeometryRefineNarrowsTo` and `TestOpenAimsTheBufferNearTwoHundredMilliseconds` pin it.

- **A write goes on where it stopped, waits bounded, and types how it ended.** `WRITEI_FRAMES` on the non-blocking node: a partial write continues from the first frame not taken; EINTR is retried; EAGAIN waits in poll for room at most `stallTimeout` (ten buffers, at least a second: `stalled`); EPIPE is an xrun, ESTRPIPE a suspend, ENODEV, ENXIO, POLLHUP and POLLNVAL a disconnect. **POLLERR alone is not a disconnect**: the kernel's poll raises it in the XRUN, SUSPENDED and SETUP states too, so the write asks STATUS what the state is, and a POLLERR the state explains away twice with no frame between is an error, not a loop. - **A cancel ends a wait at once.** The real poll waits on the PCM node and on an eventfd of its own, which `context.AfterFunc` writes when the context ends; a callback that has started is waited for before the eventfd closes, so its write never lands on a reused descriptor. - **Stopping is bounded, and the node is closed once.** Drop is DROP. Drain issues DRAIN, which the non-blocking node answers with EAGAIN, then asks STATUS at least once a period, waiting in between on a poll for no event, which only the drain's end (POLLERR in SETUP) or a fault wakes; at the deadline, or on a cancel, it drops the stream (`drain_timeout`, `cancelled`). Close is DROP and HW_FREE when HW_PARAMS had set the hardware, then close, once, whatever the kernel says to the first two; every refusal after the open leaves the same way. **Don't wait for a drain without a deadline, and don't let a refused open keep the node.** `TestDrainWaitsForTheKernelToFinish`, `TestDrainDropsAtItsDeadline`, `TestDrainTypesWhatEndsIt`, `TestCloseClosesOnEveryPath` and `TestCloseIsOnceAndThenNothingReachesTheKernel` pin it. A stream that has already stopped is not drained: `Drain` reads the state first and reports an xrun, a suspend or a disconnect, because DRAIN moves an XRUN stream to SETUP and an underrun after the last write would read as a clean end (the Claude API review and Codex on #1301; `TestDrainAfterAnUnderrunIsAnXrun`). An underrun between that check and DRAIN is erased by DRAIN too, so a stream the check saw RUNNING that reaches SETUP without being seen DRAINING is reported as an xrun (`TestDrainSeesAnUnderrunBetweenItsCheckAndDRAIN`). EBADFD that STATUS also answers is a disconnect (one PCM removed). **Don't drain without reading the state first.**

- **The device logic is portable; only the kernel seam is Linux's.** `sys` is the open, ioctl (by pointer and by value), poll and close calls; `linuxSys` is built on `linux && (amd64 || arm64)` only, the layouts the `alsa-abi` job checks, and `Open` refuses with `ReasonUnsupported` anywhere else (`TestOpenOffLinuxIsUnsupported`). - **`bridge output pcm-test` is the hardware harness, run by hand.** It is not listed in `bridge output`'s usage and is not called from the server or reachable over HTTP. ## The player on the LAN (B470 PR 5a)

- **The occurrence heard, and what the backend reports of it (B470 PR 5a3).** OpenHome's Time and Info need the length and the format of what plays, and the engine carried neither. ## Native engine hand-off (B470 2c.2a)

Since B728's B7 (the cutover) the production admission is the effective DSD policy's (`playback.Config.DSD`: `admitDSD` in `internal/player/playback/dsd_policy.go`), and the admission this section describes is the engineering harness's. What the two share is unchanged: per-occurrence accepted prefixes, one activation owner, the cancellable reopen and the fixed native pause deadlines. An activation reads the policy again (`checkDSDActivation`), and an occurrence a change of the set did not keep is refused there, never played another way.

The production playback admission refuses every DSD tuple before resolving or
opening hardware. Accepting admission and fake-kernel injection live in test
files and the existing native integration overlay. Serve and output-play use
constructors tested against valid DSF and raw DFF content. No approval, transport
selector, preference or fallback is introduced here.

An admitted occurrence retains one opaque plan: full tuple, policy ID, pause,
tail, prefill and transition edges. Compatible tuple/policy occurrences share a
sink and its carry; a different tuple drains and reopens. Native positions count
logical bits per channel at the logical bit rate, and Details reports Bits=1,
Codec=DSD, Lossless=true. Native open or activation failure faults the session.
Health, mixer-monitor and approval-record revocation producers, PCM-only epoch
publication, and approval storage remain the next phase's work. The backend
accepts a typed per-session revoke input for those future producers.

Navigation releases native content with TrackChange; Stop escalates to Immediate
on a worker so the engine owner stays responsive. The retiring ledger accepts
ordered outgoing progress independently of selected destination. Its token's
logical-bit cutoff is published after the in-flight write returns its accepted
prefix. Final progress/fault precedes successful acknowledgement. Done only says
cleanup completed: failed DROP, HW_FREE or close is ErrReleaseUnconfirmed,
faults, and cannot authorize a successor. An expected cancellation caused by
Immediate is suppressed; an established device fault is not. Late successful
cleanup is distinct from a failed cleanup.

Native pause retains the fixed 30-second release even when the engine's PCM
PauseRelease differs. A native-involved transport installed during pause re-arms
the effective deadline and invalidates any queued timer for the old transport.
Deleting an installed, unsubmitted native output revokes its activation before
admission can return and write prefill. Reopening resolves the binding afresh and admits the opened
node, retaining content and policy. Resume alone cannot activate an inert sink;
the backend supplies explicit initial activation authority for a sink installed
while paused. A shared reservation remains charged until engine and backend
both relinquish it, including stalled cancelled resolver reads; production's
bound is 1024 and tests exercise four. EOF alone does not relinquish ownership.

Qualification version 7 covers engine and playback activation/release code.
Version-6 and older reports are not attributable to this implementation. The real-ref gate
must be rerun by the reviewer after committing. Evidence and limitations are
recorded in the 2c.2a report and the engineering log; no hardware was used.
