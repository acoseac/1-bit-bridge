## Player DSD containers (B470 PR 1b)

- **Canonical native DSD is stereo, channel-separated bytes, bit 7 earliest** (`format.NativeDSD`). DSF with 1 bit per sample is LSB-first and each byte is bit-reversed; DSF with 8 is MSB-first and passes unchanged; uncompressed DFF passes unchanged. `Frames` is one-bit samples per channel and `BytesPerChannel` rounds it up: when the DSF sample count is not a multiple of eight, the last byte's trailing bits are the file's padding, passed as they are, and the transport accounts for them. `BytesPerChannel` divides before it rounds, because `(Frames+7)/8` wraps negative for a `Frames` within 7 of `math.MaxInt64`, which a DSF header can declare beside a data chunk declared large enough to pass open (round 2). It is 0 for a `Frames` of 0 or less and at most 2^60, and the DSF decoder's end is that same value, so a seek to `BytesPerChannel` is always the decoder's end. **Don't convert DSD to PCM, don't reverse a byte the container declared MSB-first, and don't round a DSD count up by adding before dividing.** `TestNativeDSDBytesPerChannel` and `TestDSFHugeSampleCountKeepsItsByteArithmetic` pin the count.

- **Accepted:** DSF version 1, format id 0 (DSD raw), channel type 2 with 2 channels, block size 4096, 1 or 8 bits per sample; DFF `FRM8`/`DSD ` whose first chunk is `FVER`, four bytes, major version 1 (DSDIFF 1.5 §3 and §3.1; the minor version is not checked), then `PROP`/`SND ` holding `FS  `, `CHNL` (`SLFT`,`SRGT` in either order: the file's order is mapped back to left, right) and `CMPR` `DSD `. Both at 2,822,400 Hz × 1, 2, 4 or 8 (DSD64..DSD512); the 48 kHz family and DSD1024 are refused. A DFF without `FVER`, with another chunk first, with a second `FVER` before the sound data, with an `FVER` that is not four bytes, or of another major version is `unsupported_layout`, and the refusal names `FVER`; a file cut inside the version is `truncated_or_corrupt`. A second `FVER` after the sound data is not refused: the walk returns at the sound chunk and never reads past it. A channel count other than 2 is `unsupported_channel_count`; DST (in `CMPR` or as a `DST ` sound chunk) and any other compression is `unsupported_compression`, never decoded. **Don't decode DST, don't take the first two channels of a multichannel file, and don't skip `FVER` as an unknown chunk.** `TestDFFFormatVersionRequired` pins `FVER`, and `TestDFFChunkBombStopsAtTheCap` that the chunk-cap fixture, which needs an `FVER` to reach the cap, is refused by the cap.

- **PR 1a's invariants hold for DSD.** Every file size is compared as `uint64` against what is addressable before any `int64` conversion, and every bound subtracts from what remains instead of adding to a position: the DFF walk asks `frmEnd-pos < 12` before it reads a chunk header and bounds each chunk with `dffChunkEnd`, because near `math.MaxInt64` a sum wraps negative and passes the check meant to stop it (round 3: crafted sizes walked a DFF to `MaxInt64-1`, where `pos+12` wrapped and the walk read a header past its form). The DSF and DFF chunk walks and PROP's sub-chunks are capped at 256, PROP's body at 64 KiB; the DFF sound chunk and every PROP sub-chunk must sit inside their parent. **Don't bound a size by adding it to a position.** `TestDFFWalkReadsNothingPastItsForm`, which records every read request because a read past the data returns EOF and looks like truncation, and `TestDFFChunkEndBoundsBySubtraction` pin it. DFF reads go through `linearRegion`, the same bounded read WAV and AIFF use (`pcmRegion` embeds it): capped at the sound data, `ctx` checked after each read, the reader seeked back on a cancel or an I/O error. DSF reads one block group's span at a time and seeks to its absolute offset before each read, so a cancel or an I/O error leaves nothing to undo; a failed seek sticks until a successful `Seek`. A file cut short returns the whole bytes before the cut (every channel the same count) with a `truncated_or_corrupt` refusal, `n > 0` with the error as the `Decoder` contract allows. **Don't add a second linear reader beside `linearRegion`.**

- **DSD goldens are an independently specified pattern**, not a decoder round trip. `scripts/player-dsd-goldens/generate.py` writes the DSF and DFF fixtures from the specifications around `pattern.bin`: no byte is a bit palindrome, left and right differ at every index, and `0x00` never occurs, so a missed reversal, a channel swap or padding read as audio each change bytes. The fixtures are marked `binary` in `.gitattributes` because `pattern.bin` has no NUL. **The DFF goldens give both chunk walks an odd chunk before the sound data**: every DFF golden has a 7-byte `MANF` before `PROP` for the form's walk, since §3.6 pads a `COMT` comment inside its body, so a `COMT` is always even; and every DFF golden but `dst.dff`, a refusal fixture whose `CMPR` ("DST Encoded") is 16 bytes, has the 19-byte `CMPR` of §3.2.3 for `PROP`'s walk. The two accepted DFF goldens, `dsd64.dff` and `dsd256_right_first.dff`, have both. Until round 2 the generator padded `CMPR` inside its size and `COMT` outside it, every `PROP` sub-chunk was even, and a `PROP` walk without its pad skip passed every test. **Don't make every chunk of a DFF golden even.** The demuxer skips `COMT` and `MANF` by their sizes, so no decoding test sees their layout: `TestDFFGoldensCOMTFollowsSection36` and `TestDFFGoldensGiveTheWalksAnOddChunk` read the committed bytes instead (round 3). To check a generator change without touching the committed fixtures, write into a scratch directory with `generate.py --out DIR` and run `go test ./internal/player/decode -args -dsd-goldens=DIR`. `TestDSDGoldensMatchPattern`, `TestDSDGoldensExactSeek`, `TestDSDGoldensEOFAtSampleCount`, `TestDSDGoldensRefused`, `TestDSDTruncatedMidBlock`, `TestDSDAfterID3Prefix`, `TestDSDAbsurdSizesRefused`, `TestDSFMetadataPointerIgnored`, `TestDSDCancelThenRetryNoGap`, `TestDSDIOErrorThenRetryNoGap`, `TestDSDReadBufferShape`, `FuzzDSFOpen` and `FuzzDFFOpen` pin it.

- **The sample count ends DSF audio, never the data size.** The final block group is zero-padded to 4096 per channel; the data chunk must hold every block group the sample count needs, or the file is `truncated_or_corrupt` at open. The `DSD ` chunk's metadata pointer and total size are never used, so ID3 metadata is never read as audio. Before the sound data, DFF chunks other than `FVER` and `PROP` (`COMT`, `MANF`, and `DIIN` or `ID3 ` where a writer puts them there) are skipped by their sizes plus the pad byte after an odd body (§2.3), in the form's walk and in `PROP`'s; `PROP` must precede the sound data. The walk validates chunk headers through the form end, seeking over audio and metadata bodies; a second PROP after sound, trailing property bytes, missing padding or bytes outside the form refuse playback. `CMPR` is read only for its four-byte compressionType, so a `CMPR` whose size counts the name's pad (20 bytes for "not compressed", the form `internal/dsdtone` writes) opens like §3.2.3's 19-byte one (`TestDFFCMPRWithItsNamePadInsideStillOpens`). **Don't follow the DSF metadata pointer.**

## Player ALAC in M4A (B470 PR 1c)

- **Carry the patched saprobe-alac in `third_party/saprobe-alac`, through `go.mod`'s replace, until a released saprobe-alac checks every entropy code against the packet's own length** (2026-10-21, backlog B470). 1b6eb83's `DynDecomp` checked its cursor only before each sample, so a packet's last code decoded from the four zero bytes of padding after it with no error, or read past the padding and panicked (Codex's review of #1300; `ops/upstream/saprobe-alac.md`, 1 and 7). The copy checks an escape's value before reading it (which also bounds a 32-bit pair's 33-bit read), a normal code's length before taking it, and a zero run's count before and after `dynGet`, and returns the library's `ErrBitstreamOverrun`. `ops/upstream/saprobe-alac.patch` is that change as a diff against 1b6eb83; the copy's `README.md` lists every change, and its patched `golomb.go` says in its header that it was changed (Apache-2.0 §4(b)). The Dockerfile copies the copy's `go.mod` before `go mod download`, which cannot build the module graph without it. **Don't drop the replace or the patch, and don't bump saprobe-alac past them until a release carries that fix; then remove the replace and `third_party/saprobe-alac` in the same change.** `TestALACCodesPastThePacketAreRefused`, `TestSaprobeALACRefusesCodesPastThePacket` and `TestALACPanicPacketIsRefusedWithoutTheRecover` pin it, and `go -C third_party/saprobe-alac test ./...` runs the copy's own tests, which `make test` does not reach (the copy is a module of its own).
  **Retired 2026-10-23.** Upstream published GHSA-g66f-c97f-q276 (acoseac credited) and tagged v1.0.0-rc3 (9c424f2e; 3087cde0 checks every code against the packet in `DynDecomp` and validates the cookie in `NewPacketDecoder`, 2478d2f2 bounds the container's tables), so the bridge moved to rc3 and removed the `replace` and `third_party/saprobe-alac` in one change, as this entry required. The bridge's decode tests pass on rc3, and the over-read tests go red on 1b6eb83 with the bridge's patch reversed (the PR's negative control). The rule's line in `.claude/rules/player.md` now pins rc3 as the floor.

- **A panic in the library is recovered as a `truncated_or_corrupt` refusal, the last resort**: nothing of the packet is returned, and the library decoder is discarded and constructed again for the next packet. saprobe-alac 1b6eb83 panicked on a malformed packet that every check passes, under Apple's own configuration: its entropy decoder's zero-run read ran past the padded buffer (`alacPanicPacket`, ten bytes). That met B470's trigger for porting Apple's reference decoder (option C); the owner kept option B, the maintainers were told privately first, and on 2026-10-21 the owner chose to carry a patched copy (the bullet on the copy), which refuses that packet with its own overrun error, so no known packet reaches the recover. **Don't remove the recover, and don't let a recovered packet return samples.** `TestALACInjectedPanicIsARefusal` pins the recover with an injected panic, `TestALACPanicPacketIsRefusedWithoutTheRecover` that the real packet is refused without it, and `TestALACLibraryErrorIsARefusal` that a packet the library refuses returns nothing and the packets after it still decode.

- **ALAC goldens are an independently specified pattern that afconvert encodes.** `scripts/player-alac-goldens/generate.py` writes it, decodes every encode back with afconvert and writes none that is not the pattern, and zeroes the timestamps afconvert stamps, so a rerun is byte-identical; `alacPattern` reproduces it in Go and checks the manifest's hash. The goldens are 16-bit/44.1 kHz, 24-bit/96 kHz, 24-bit/192 kHz with a one-frame last packet, 32-bit/44.1 kHz, a moov-last co64 variant and an edited variant, refusals (a real 20-bit ALAC, mono, AAC), and two FFmpeg encodes from Codex's evaluation, whose expected audio is the hash of the PCM Codex encoded. Every golden decodes escape and compressed packets (`TestALACGoldensDecodeBothPacketKinds`). FFmpeg's byte hashes cannot see a missing sign extension, which packing drops; the pattern goldens compare int32 values. To check a generator change without touching the fixtures, `generate.py --out DIR` and `go test ./internal/player/decode -args -alac-goldens=DIR`. `TestALACReferenceCorpus` compares the private corpus and Codex's encodes with FFmpeg's hashes, whole and from eleven seek frames each, when they are present. **Don't derive an ALAC expectation from the decoder.**

- **The M4A demuxer (`m4a.go`, `alac.go`) reads boxes by seeking, never moov whole**, so cover art in moov costs a seek. It reads ftyp first, one moov anywhere (before or after mdat), every mdat, and the one `soun` track: mdhd, hdlr, dinf/dref (one self-contained `url ` or `urn ` entry), stsd, stts, stsc, stsz, stco or co64, and edts/elst. Fragmented files (`moof`, `mfra`, `mvex`), several audio tracks, an external data reference, `stz2`, `ctts`, several sample descriptions, a QuickTime sound description (version 1 or 2), and a second box of any type it reads are refused. Caps: 256 boxes a container, 4096 an open, 4 KiB for the small boxes read whole, 64 KiB for stsd, 2^20 packets, and 1024 stts runs once entries of one duration side by side are merged, refused past that as `unsupported_layout` (every golden, corpus file and FFmpeg encode has one run or two). The index is twelve bytes a packet and 32 a run, 12 MiB and 32 KiB at the caps: stsc is read in step with the chunk offsets and never held, so it has no cap, and each table is read 4096 entries at a time (`TestALACDemuxerAllocatesLittle`, `TestALACSTTSRunsMergeAndCap`). **Every table count is checked against its box with `fits` before anything is allocated for it, every box against its parent by subtraction (PR 1b round 3), and a constant-size stsz's count against the mdat bytes the file holds.** A top-level mdat may run past the end of a cut file, so the packets before the cut play; any other box past its parent is `unsupported_layout` (what the sniffer says of the same box), and a top-level box past the end of the file is `truncated_or_corrupt`. The tables must agree: stts and stsc place exactly stsz's packets, every stts duration is between 1 and the frame length, every packet is 1 byte to the cookie's max frame bytes (or the format's bound) and lies inside one mdat, mdhd's timescale is the cookie's rate, and mdhd's duration is stts's frames. **Don't load moov whole, don't allocate from a count before `fits`, and don't add a table reader that skips it.** `TestALACAbsurdTablesRefused`, `TestALACBoxSizesRefused`, `TestALACDemuxerAlone`, `TestALACHostileTablesAllocateLittle` and `TestALACDemuxerAllocatesLittle` pin it.

## Player ALSA PCM backend (B470 PR 2a)

- **The kernel interface is pinned and checked, never vendored.** `internal/alsapcm/uapi.go` holds the parts of `include/uapi/sound/asound.h` the backend uses, as a 64-bit little-endian process sees them (linux/amd64 and linux/arm64), from the tag `uapiKernel` names (`v6.12`). `scripts/alsa-abi/check.sh` fetches the header at that tag, compiles `probe.c` against it with the machine's own compiler, runs it, and runs `TestUAPIMatchesTheKernelHeader` on its output: every size, offset, field size, interval bit, constant and ioctl number. - **An endpoint is two numbers.** `alsapcm.Endpoint{Card, Device}` names `/dev/snd/pcmC<card>D<device>p` and nothing else: there is no string form, so `default`, `hw:`, `plughw:`, dmix, softvol, an alias or a path cannot reach `Open`, and no ALSA configuration is read. - **Rate, channels and format are exact; the buffer geometry may vary.** The request (RW_INTERLEAVED, the format, STD, two channels, the rate) is refined exactly; a refusal is `parameters_not_supported`, naming the formats, channels and rates the hardware offers. After HW_PARAMS the committed access, format, channels and rate are read back: each must be the one asked for, a committed interval is read only as a closed single value (the kernel's own runtime takes its minimum), and `rate_num` must be the rate times `rate_den`. Anything else is `parameters_mismatch`, and the open frees the parameters and closes the node: nothing is taken in its place. The buffer is aimed at `DefaultBufferTime` (200 ms) in four periods, each tried exactly and then as a range, and `Params` reports what was granted. **Don't accept a committed value other than the one asked for, and don't read a committed interval any other way.** `TestOpenCommitsExactlyWhatWasAsked`, `TestOpenRefusesWhatTheHardwareDoesNotTake`, `TestOpenRefusesAReadBackThatIsNotWhatWasAsked`, `TestOpenTakesTheGeometryRefineNarrowsTo` and `TestOpenAimsTheBufferNearTwoHundredMilliseconds` pin it.

- **A write goes on where it stopped, waits bounded, and types how it ended.** `WRITEI_FRAMES` on the non-blocking node: a partial write continues from the first frame not taken; EINTR is retried; EAGAIN waits in poll for room at most `stallTimeout` (ten buffers, at least a second: `stalled`); EPIPE is an xrun, ESTRPIPE a suspend, ENODEV, ENXIO, POLLHUP and POLLNVAL a disconnect. **POLLERR alone is not a disconnect**: the kernel's poll raises it in the XRUN, SUSPENDED and SETUP states too, so the write asks STATUS what the state is, and a POLLERR the state explains away twice with no frame between is an error, not a loop. - **A cancel ends a wait at once.** The real poll waits on the PCM node and on an eventfd of its own, which `context.AfterFunc` writes when the context ends; a callback that has started is waited for before the eventfd closes, so its write never lands on a reused descriptor. - **Stopping is bounded, and the node is closed once.** Drop is DROP. Drain issues DRAIN, which the non-blocking node answers with EAGAIN, then asks STATUS at least once a period, waiting in between on a poll for no event, which only the drain's end (POLLERR in SETUP) or a fault wakes; at the deadline, or on a cancel, it drops the stream (`drain_timeout`, `cancelled`). A stream that has already stopped is not drained: `Drain` reads the state first and reports an xrun, a suspend or a disconnect, because DRAIN moves an XRUN stream to SETUP and an underrun after the last write would read as a clean end (the Claude API review and Codex on #1301; `TestDrainAfterAnUnderrunIsAnXrun`). An underrun between that check and DRAIN is erased by DRAIN too, so a stream the check saw RUNNING that reaches SETUP without being seen DRAINING is reported as an xrun (`TestDrainSeesAnUnderrunBetweenItsCheckAndDRAIN`). EBADFD that STATUS also answers is a disconnect (one PCM removed). **Don't drain without reading the state first.**

- **The device logic is portable; only the kernel seam is Linux's.** `sys` is the open, ioctl (by pointer and by value), poll and close calls; `linuxSys` is built on `linux && (amd64 || arm64)` only, the layouts the `alsa-abi` job checks, and `Open` refuses with `ReasonUnsupported` anywhere else (`TestOpenOffLinuxIsUnsupported`). - **`bridge output pcm-test` is the hardware harness, run by hand.** It is not listed in `bridge output`'s usage and is not called from the server or reachable over HTTP. It opens an endpoint by number, prints what the kernel committed and compares it with the kernel's own `hw_params` file, refusing to play on a difference; plays a 1 kHz sine at most -30 dBFS (`--level` only goes quieter) on both channels, packed by `format.Pack`, in whole repeats so the waveform has no seam, with `--rate` held to `alsapcm.MinRateHz`..`MaxRateHz` before the tone is built (its table is as long as a rate that shares no factor with 1000); reports the delay each second, the writes, waits and any xrun; drains and closes. ### Round 12: one remedy for a directory failure, a file's own access for a file

Round 11's advice named the access a step needed, and three kinds of failure
named one the user already had. An Lstat, which needs the directory's search
access, said read; the marker write's directory sync, in a directory the user
may write and search but not read, said write; and the lock file's open, for
reading and writing, said write access to `server.lock`, also where the file
was missing and the open had failed creating it. A remedy is advice the
operator follows, not a diagnosis, so every failure at the data directory
itself now takes one remedy that always suffices (`directoryAccess`): on unix,
restore read, write and search (execute) access to the data directory and each
directory above it, or clear an immutable flag or ACL on it; on Windows,
restore full control of the data directory. These failures are an entry's
Lstat (in `markerEntry`, `playerEntryNotAFile`, `playerAllocationEvidence` and
the lock file's check), the barrier, which opens the directory to sync it and
creates nothing, and a durable write's temporary file, rename and directory
sync. `ensurePlayerMarker` now takes the entry's kind from the one Lstat that
read the marker (`markerEntry`), so it has no Lstat of its own.

A file's own open names the file (`fileAccess`). A marker's open or read
needs read access to it; an EPERM rather than an EACCES adds "or clear an
immutable flag or ACL on it", which read access alone does not undo. The lock
file is opened for reading and writing and created when missing, so a
permission failure there is told apart by an Lstat through the data
directory's root, which follows nothing outside it (`lockFileAccess`). A
regular file needs read and write access to it, with the same EPERM addition.
No entry, a link the root resolves to nothing, a root that will not open and
an Lstat that fails take the directory remedy: the open failed creating the
file, or never reached it. Any other entry keeps the lock file's write remedy.
The claim that serve boot and start fail on it too holds when they run as this
user, as before.

On the owner's Mac a backup's own lock open needs to read the data directory
(`os.OpenRoot`), so in a 0300 directory it fails there, with the directory
remedy, before completion reaches the seal's barrier; the barrier case drives
the completion itself. The identity-free restore of a snapshot whose
`player-uuid` entry is damaged is now pinned to its one outcome: it completes,
and the box's identity and floor stay byte for byte. Round 10's failed-write
case now asserts the directory remedy.

## Native engine hand-off (B470 2c.2a)

The production playback admission refuses every DSD tuple before resolving or
opening hardware. Accepting admission and fake-kernel injection live in test
files and the existing native integration overlay. Serve and output-play use
constructors tested against valid DSF and raw DFF content. No approval, transport
selector, preference or fallback is introduced here.

An admitted occurrence retains one opaque plan: full tuple, policy ID, pause,
tail, prefill and transition edges. Compatible tuple/policy occurrences share a
sink and its carry; a different tuple drains and reopens. Native positions count
logical bits per channel at the logical bit rate, and Details reports Bits=1,
Codec=DSD, Lossless=true. Native open or activation failure faults the session.
Health, mixer-monitor and approval-record revocation producers, PCM-only epoch
publication, and approval storage remain the next phase's work. The backend
accepts a typed per-session revoke input for those future producers.

Navigation releases native content with TrackChange; Stop escalates to Immediate
on a worker so the engine owner stays responsive. The retiring ledger accepts
ordered outgoing progress independently of selected destination. Its token's
logical-bit cutoff is published after the in-flight write returns its accepted
prefix. Final progress/fault precedes successful acknowledgement. Done only says
cleanup completed: failed DROP, HW_FREE or close is ErrReleaseUnconfirmed,
faults, and cannot authorize a successor. An expected cancellation caused by
Immediate is suppressed; an established device fault is not. Late successful
cleanup is distinct from a failed cleanup.

Native pause retains the fixed 30-second release even when the engine's PCM
PauseRelease differs. A native-involved transport installed during pause re-arms
the effective deadline and invalidates any queued timer for the old transport.
Deleting an installed, unsubmitted native output revokes its activation before
admission can return and write prefill. Reopening resolves the binding afresh and admits the opened
node, retaining content and policy. Resume alone cannot activate an inert sink;
the backend supplies explicit initial activation authority for a sink installed
while paused. A shared reservation remains charged until engine and backend
both relinquish it, including stalled cancelled resolver reads; production's
bound is 1024 and tests exercise four. EOF alone does not relinquish ownership.

Qualification version 7 covers engine and playback activation/release code.
Version-6 and older reports are not attributable to this implementation. The real-ref gate
must be rerun by the reviewer after committing. Evidence and limitations are
recorded in the 2c.2a report and the engineering log; no hardware was used.


### Native engine hand-off, review round 2 (2026-10-27)

A successful software cut completes that occurrence and continues the producer.
Installation and resume share the adapter's initial activation authority; pause
reconciliation cannot activate generation 1 twice. Reopen Locate observes both
the resume request and sink lifetime; cancelling an in-flight activation still
revokes it so the abandoned caller cannot authorize sound later.

CleanupResult is separate from Result: a playback/admission error faults and
stops the session, but successful DROP/HW_FREE/close permits later explicit play.
An early unconfirmed release stops its timer, clears heard state, and retains
only one fault for that session. The bounded retiring ledger remains available
for late accounting until acknowledgement; it never supplies queue intent.
Fault-initiated release carries ReleaseFault.

The final cutoff supplies each occurrence's start and accepted end in its own
output's coordinates. Details/Audible require a positive accepted interval;
Position is compared with that interval's length, never a sink-wide count.
Tail silence already passes through run.contentAmong and adds no content time.
A report crossing several tiny native occurrences publishes each one's measured
position before the following Audible; PCM reports are unchanged.

Unclassified reservations carry ownership but do not charge the native limit.
Native lookahead reserves before allocation; a newly identified native source
must promote its reservation before allocating native buffers. Both owners must
relinquish every charged reservation. PCM retains its prior preparation behavior,
including lookahead beyond the native limit. Between transports the backend
keeps the last installed transport's effective pause deadline, matching the
engine even with a different configured PCM pause release.

The native-qualification job runs both CLI and backend overlay race suites and
checks that tests ran; the repository wiring test and build-site census pin it.
The production-constructor test registers drainLoopOnCleanup at its launch,
before backend Wait and temporary-directory removal. It previously joined the
engine in a custom cleanup, which the repository's required helper audit rejected.
