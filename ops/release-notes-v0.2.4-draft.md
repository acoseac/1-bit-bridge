## The bridge's own player

**The bridge's own player plays DSD by default, with no approval or first-use step** (backlog B728, B990): on Linux with `output.player` on, a DAC with no DSD choice of its own takes the bridge's default, `auto`, which is `off` instead where an earlier version's opt-out carried over (below) or while the player cannot read its DSD state file. Under `auto` the bridge decides DSD64, DSD128 and DSD256 each from what its DAC catalog says of the DAC, what the DAC offers, listeners' answers and any DoP confirmation on the DAC's current firmware, for DoP the level controls on its playback path, and the choice's cap: native DSD or DoP where the rule admits one, otherwise conversion to PCM (below). Section 3 of `ops/plan-b728-dsd-capability-design.md` holds the full rule. Earlier versions played DSD only under an owner's approval, and never converted it.

**Under `auto`, DSD the DAC can't take natively or as DoP is converted to PCM** (backlog B728): to the highest of 176.4, 88.2 and 44.1 kHz the DAC takes in two channels at 24 bits, or, where it takes none of them at 24 bits, the highest at 16 bits with TPDF dither, at unity gain, and never called bit-exact; for a DAC that takes none of them, those rates are refused (`conversion_not_offered`). The OpenHome renderer now lists DSF and DFF for controllers when the player can play at least one DSD rate, converted ones included.

**DSD settings from an earlier version carry over when the player first starts** (backlog B728): for a DAC an earlier version approved rates for, what carries over is written once the player finds that DAC: its playback mode, and any rate approvals, acknowledgements and DoP confirmation the migration records, which are tied to the firmware the DAC ran when they were approved and count only while it runs that firmware; an explicit `output.player.dsdMode: off`, or `dop` without `allowDoP`, becomes the default, off, for every DAC with no choice of its own; with none of `dsdMode`, `allowDoP` or `dsdQualification` set, DSD plays under `auto` from the first start. Those three keys reach the player's DSD state only through this migration, and each start of serve warns of each one set. Each DAC's choice is kept in `player-dsd.json` in the data directory, which backups carry.

**The bridge publishes each DAC's DSD choice, and an API changes it while playback is stopped** (backlog B728): `GET /v1/outputs` describes the bound DAC's DSD (`dsdDAC`, and in `dsdPlayback` the rates that play native or as DoP, those converted and those refused, with why), and `PUT /v1/outputs/{id}/dsd` (feature `bridgeDSDChoice`) sets its mode to `auto`, `native`, `dop`, `convert` or `off`, with an optional rate cap (`maxBitRate`) and DoP confirmation (`confirmDoP`), refused with "Stop playback to change DSD" while anything plays; while a write runs, the player starts nothing. `dsdPlayback` no longer sends `qualificationRevision` or `qualifiedOn`, which the app already read as optional, and the wire protocol stays at version 1. No released app uses the change route yet.

**The console's Player page plays a test tone** (backlog B601): a 1 kHz sine on both channels, at -30 to -60 dBFS for 5, 10 or 30 seconds, through the DAC while the player is stopped; turn the DAC and the amplifier down first, since the bridge can't see their volume. Stop or leaving the page ends it, and a play from the app or another controller stops it.

**The Player page's transports table follows the DAC's DSD choice** (backlog B1028, B1029): each DSD row is set by the DSD choice that applies to the DAC (its own, which `PUT /v1/outputs/{id}/dsd` changes, or the bridge's default for a DAC with none), the Approved column is now Rates, the rates each way plays now, and a fourth row lists the DSD rates converted to PCM, with the PCM each becomes. The page no longer says DSD comes in a later version, and it still has no DSD control. The example config's player paragraph says the same.

**A track that fails before it is heard no longer sends Play back to the track before it** (backlog B903AUD): in a gapless queue, when the next track fails before any of it was heard, playback still stops, and Play from Stopped now starts the entry after the track heard instead of replaying that track. With nothing after it (the last track, repeat off), the selection stays where it was.

## The web console

**The console's player bar plays to the bridge's own player** (backlog B634): its output button picks this browser or the bridge's player, so the bridge plays to its DAC with no app, wherever the player is on. With the bridge's player picked, the bar plays, pauses, stops, skips, seeks within PCM (not DSD, and not in a window 768 px wide or less), repeats the queue or nothing, and edits the queue as the app does; shuffle, repeat-one and volume are not offered, since the level is fixed. A switch to this browser first pauses the bridge's player and waits up to 10 seconds for it to fall silent.

**The console can also be opened on the home network, as an explicit opt-in** (backlog B989): on a loopback install, `adminLANAddress` (or `BRIDGE_ADMIN_LAN_ADDRESS`, or `bridge init --admin-lan-address`) opens it on one private address of the box, at the console's port, with no login and over plain HTTP, to devices on that address's own network (Tailscale's ranges excluded): anyone there can watch it and change the bridge's settings, music and devices. In public mode the bridge refuses that address and does not start. Linux needs kernel 5.7 or later (or `CAP_NET_RAW`) and macOS takes an IPv4 address only; where the listener can't be held to its interface, the console stays on this machine and says why. While the key is in `bridge.yaml`, even empty, 0.2.3 and earlier refuse the file, so an update's automatic rollback to one is not made and one installed by hand does not start: remove the line before going back (the environment variable costs nothing there).

**The console works by touch on phones and tablets** (backlog B1021): with a coarse pointer the player bar's buttons, the Output menu's picks, the queue's entries and Remove buttons, and the breadcrumb links are at least 44 px tall, a window 768 px wide or less gets both times in the player bar, read-only, with no seek, the A–Z rail fits between the bars, and no page runs sideways. Controls that can't act say why in words (the Output menu says what the bridge's player doesn't do and what the DAC is handed), and the bar shows the album cover for both outputs.

**A DSD track says where it plays** (backlog B1021): with this browser as the output, a DSD row the bridge's player can play shows its rate and how it would play there (native, DoP or converted to PCM), and an album's Play, Shuffle and Add are disabled, with the reason in words, when nothing in it can play in the current output.

**An album held in several editions shows one at a time** (backlog B1023): an album whose folders hold the same songs (a DSD, a hi-res FLAC and a CD folder, say) listed every copy of every song interleaved, and Play, Shuffle and Add took them all. Its page now has a picker with each edition's format and track count, starting on the best edition the chosen output plays, and Play, Shuffle and Add act on the edition shown. Delete still moves the files of every edition (backlog B1036): its confirmation names, whichever edition is shown, each edition whose files it moves, with how many, and each it leaves alone (an SACD image's, or one on an upstream server).

**A Settings save keeps the settings another writer changed in `bridge.yaml`** (backlog B535): the console merges the settings it changed into the file and keeps the ones a hand edit or a CLI command changed, rather than writing its own copy over theirs, or refuses the save, with nothing saved, and says why and what to do.

**The upload page runs one upload at a time** (backlog B797): while an upload, a resume or a retry runs, the pickers, drops, Upload, Try again and Discard are refused and the drop zone says why, so two uploads no longer disturb each other's progress or files.

**The console's lists keep your place and filter by name** (backlog B1021): Back to a list, or its crumb, returns to the row you left, past the first page and after a jump on the index rail too, and a list restored past its top offers Back to the top. Albums (a genre's and a composer's albums included), Artists, Genres, Composers and Playlists take a filter that narrows the list by name (an album by its title or its album artist), its count, rail and pages with it. In Safari, a tap during or just after a long scroll no longer goes nowhere.

**Every album sort has an index rail, and a finger can drag it** (backlog B1021): Recently Added, the sort Albums opens on, gets a rail by date added (this month, earlier this year, then each year), Year one by year or by decade, and Title and Artist keep their letters, as the artists' and composers' lists do. On a touch screen the rail is one column at least 44 px wide, as tall as the room between the bars, which takes a drag and shows where the list is in a callout beside the finger.

## Setup, CLI and doctor

**An update counts as healthy only once it has answered and stayed up for 10 minutes** (backlog B660): until then the install stays on probation with `.bak` kept, and after three starts of the new version that did not end cleanly, the next start puts the previous version back and exits 75 for the service manager to start it; where it may not, `bridge doctor` fails and the Probation row in Settings → Updates says why. A migration of the database rules that rollback out, and this release migrates it the first time it opens it (schema 60 to 62): the upgrade notes below say the way back.

**On Windows the updater waits out a moment's hold on the bridge's binary** (backlog B898): a real-time scanner holding the binary could leave a failing release in place after a rollback, or no binary at all; each rename is now retried for a few seconds on a sharing or lock violation, never on access denied. `bridge restore` waits out a busy snapshot the same way, and refuses one still busy by asking you to run it again shortly (exit 1), rather than calling it unrecognized (exit 2).

**On a Windows Service install, `bridge logs` and `bridge doctor` read the Service's own log** (backlog B618): run from a shell, they looked under the shell user's folder, so they read or graded a log the bridge never writes; where the install kind can't be told, the doctor says the log wasn't checked.

**Snapshots are verified as they are taken** (backlog B653): each snapshot records every file's size and SHA-256, and a check gives its database (SQLite's `quick_check` and its schema) and its other files a verdict each, which `bridge doctor` and the console (Jobs, Settings → Backups) show; `bridge backup --verify <snapshot-dir> [--full]` checks one again, and `bridge backup` now exits 0 only when both verdicts are `VERIFIED`. Retention keeps the newest snapshot whose database verified beside the `keep` newest. Checks and prunes need a file lock in the backups folder: on storage that grants none, no check, prune or scheduled snapshot runs while the newest waits for its check (`bridge doctor`'s `backup-lock` line says so), so keep the data directory on a local disk or a share whose lock service runs.

**`bridge backup` prunes by the config's retention** (backlog B1035): without `--keep` it now keeps what `backup.keep` (or `BRIDGE_BACKUP_KEEP`) says, as serve's scheduled snapshots and the console's do: `-1` keeps every snapshot, 0 or none the default 7, and `--keep N` overrides it for one run (0 or less keeps every one). Before, it kept the seven newest whatever the config said, so where the config keeps more, or every one, a snapshot taken before the update with 0.2.3's `bridge backup` takes `--keep 0`.

**`bridge restore` refuses while anything has the library open** (backlog B653, B826): it refuses, with nothing restored, while any bridge command (`scan`, `analyze`, `upscale`, `library add` or `remove`, `backup`, `doctor`) has the database open, while a check or prune of those snapshots still holds them after a short wait, and on a data directory whose storage grants no file locks; stop every process of an earlier version first, since those take no lock restore can see. A snapshot on read-only media, on storage without file locks or in a folder you can't write is refused with nothing restored: copy its folder to a local disk and restore from the copy.

**`bridge doctor` no longer hangs on a library root whose mount stopped answering** (backlog B650): each step that touches a root gets 5 seconds, a root that doesn't answer fails `library-roots` by its configured name, and every other check finishes, in the CLI and the console's doctor alike.

**The doctors, `bridge init`, the console and the log say when `bridge.yaml` or the data directory lies inside a library root** (backlog B884): a library root is often a mount, and while one stops answering, every command that reads inside it waits on it, serve and the doctor included. Nothing is refused; on a managed bridge the doctor and the Diagnostics page leave the warning out.

**`bridge init` that keeps a config prepares the data directory first** (backlog B664): before it installs or starts the service, it checks the data directory the service will use, as serve does, and makes it, and where serve would refuse it exits non-zero with the reason, the config unchanged. Before, the service failed at its working directory (systemd's 200/CHDIR, restarting every 2 seconds) while init exited 0.

**Commands refuse a data directory whose volume reads as away** (backlog B727, B741): every command that writes the data directory now refuses it, exit 1, as serve does, when the recorded data directory reads as not mounted; reads still run, and a data directory moved on purpose needs `dataDir`. Serve also writes an identity into the data directory, `data-dir-id.json`: leave it there, move it with the data directory, and run commands as the user the service runs as. A data directory refused for holding no identity that is the right one, its volume mounted, is taken as serve's own again once `.bridge.yaml.datadir` beside the config is removed.

**A config lock the bridge can't use names its remedy** (backlog B537): a `.bridge.yaml.lock` owned by another user, or with a mode that refuses reading and writing, now says what to do: give it the config file's owner with owner read and write, or remove it while nothing writes the config.

**`bridge init --force` keeps the enrichment settings, and a Settings save keeps what only the environment changed** (backlog B617, B761): a rewrite keeps non-default `enrich.musicbrainzBaseURL` and `enrich.coverArtBaseURL`, and the enrichment switches written off. A console save no longer writes a `BRIDGE_*` variable's value into `bridge.yaml` for a setting left as it was, and Settings says the environment wins at the next start.

## Networking and the app

**The embedded Tailscale node uploads no diagnostic logs unless the config allows it** (backlog B749): in `tsnet` mode the bridge sets `TS_NO_LOGS_NO_SUPPORT=true` for its node unless `tailscale.uploadDiagnosticLogs: true` is set (in `bridge.yaml`, or Settings, Networking), so an install updated from an earlier version stops uploading Tailscale's diagnostic logs, and network flow logs, at its first start of this version: earlier versions uploaded them by default, which is not taken for consent. The node keeps its identity and its state. A tailnet that requires those logs refuses the node until the key is set, and the admin tile says what to change, and, where the key is set and the uploads are off all the same, what holds them off. A `TS_NO_LOGS_NO_SUPPORT=true` in the bridge's own environment keeps the uploads off even with the key set, so allowing them takes removing it as well. A change of the key takes a restart of the bridge's process: once the bridge has run its node with the key off, the uploads stay off until that process exits, so quit the bridge, its menu included, and launch it again, or restart its service (the menu's Start now, and the start `bridge init` offers, run the bridge in the same process). A stop of the bridge that gives up waiting for its node's start, with the setting on, turns the uploads off until that process exits too: no new upload starts after it, and the admin tile says so (backlog B830). The node's name no longer follows the library's: a node already registered keeps the name it has, so a library rename no longer renames it, and a new node registers as `1-bit-bridge` unless `tailscale.hostname` is set.

**An empty Tailscale state file stops the embedded node with the bridge's own explanation** (backlog B828): in `tsnet` mode a `tailscaled.state` that is empty, or holds only white space, as a truncation leaves it, made the node's start fail in Tailscale's words about a TPM-sealed state file. The bridge now refuses that start before it builds the node, whatever `tailscale.hostname` says, and serve's output, `bridge tsnet auth`, `bridge tsnet status` and the admin tile say what to do: put back a copy of the file to keep the node, or move the file aside and restart the bridge, after which the node starts as a new device that authenticates again, takes a new tailnet address and can take a new MagicDNS name. The bridge moves and replaces nothing itself, and a missing state file still starts a new node.

**Enrichment can be switched off** (backlog B617): `enrich.enabled: false` (Settings, or `BRIDGE_ENRICH_ENABLED`) stops within a second every MusicBrainz, Cover Art Archive and Deezer request, a mirror's included, the Atlas premium lookup and AcoustID fingerprinting; `enrich.deezerEnabled: false` (or `BRIDGE_ENRICH_DEEZER_ENABLED`) stops the Deezer artist photos alone. What was fetched is still served, and the Atlas harvest, lyrics, the update check and Let's Encrypt go on. While enrichment is off, a cover or artist photo still waiting for enrichment answers 404 `no_image` instead of 202 `pending`, and manifests carry no `enrichmentProgress`.

**The update check can be switched off** (backlog B761): `update.checkEnabled: false` (Settings → Updates' Check for updates, or `BRIDGE_UPDATE_CHECK_ENABLED=false`) stops every request to GitHub's releases API and every install (Check now, Install, `bridge update`, auto-install), while the status, Roll back, a start's rollback and a new version's probation still run. `/v1/health` advertises no update while it is off, and `bridge update` reads the key as the service does, so an off set only in the service's environment must be in the shell's too.

**The bridge indexes WavPack and Monkey's Audio files and serves them as they are** (backlog B489): `.wv` files (a hybrid one's `.wvc` correction file kept as part of it, never a track of its own) and `.ape` files are scanned, tagged and served unchanged, never decoded or transcoded, and the manifest and search list them only to an app that declares it plays them (`X-Bridge-Formats: wavpack, ape`; iOS 2.3), so today's app doesn't index them. Analysis, upscaling, DLNA, the console's player and the bridge's own player leave them out for now.

**A Christmas smart mix** (backlog B750): a mix named Christmas is served from 1 December to 5 January (UTC) for a library holding at least 15 Christmas tracks across 3 albums or 3 artists, found by genre, album title, carol titles and Christmas words in a title, never by artist. The console marks it out of season the rest of the year, and an app shows it as any other mix, which its Settings can hide.

**Host names that aren't ASCII are used in their IDNA form** (backlog B744): a `customEndpoints` entry or `autocert.domain` that isn't ASCII is now certified and published in its `xn--` form, `bridge init --domain` writes that form and says so, and a name that has none is refused; before, the certificate could not be made. A library name also loses its control characters wherever it is set.

**The embedded Tailscale node moves to 1.104.5, among the dependency updates**: Tailscale 1.104.2 to 1.104.5, the SQLite driver 1.61.0 to 1.62.0, quic-go 0.64.1 to 0.65.0, golang.org/x/crypto 0.58.0 to 0.59.0 and golang.org/x/image 0.47.0 to 0.48.0, with x/term and x/mod; Go stays 1.27.3. Tailscale 1.104.5's netstack turns RACK loss recovery off unless `TS_NETSTACK_ENABLE_RACK=true` is set, which may slow recovery for tsnet transfers on paths that reorder packets.

## Fixes

**The first scan after the update reads every file's tags again** (backlog B743, B489, B428): ExtractorVersion moves from 30 to 33, so files already indexed are re-read once, and rows that come out unchanged are not sent to apps again.

**A track number written with a total is read** (backlog B743): a Vorbis `TRACKNUMBER` or `DISCNUMBER` (a FLAC's, say) written `1/2`, `3 of 12` or ` 3 ` was indexed as 0, so its album played out of order; it now reads the leading number, and an ID3 tag's ` 3 ` is read too.

**An album's tracks in one folder share one year** (backlog B756): an album whose tracks carried different recording years showed as several albums in the app; each track not at its folder's most common year now takes it (a tie goes to the later year), and editions in separate folders stay apart. The first full scan after the update rewrites the rows, and serve runs one at its start.

**An M4A's work tag is read** (backlog B428): the bridge now reads `©wrk`, and a freeform `WORK` under `com.apple.iTunes`, as the iOS app does, so a file the app groups under its work over SMB groups the same way from the bridge.

**A lost waveform can be drawn again when it is asked for** (backlog B652): a curve gone from the waveform directory stayed missing for good (`/v1/waveform` answered 410). Now that request, which still answers 410, can ask for the curve to be rendered again in the background while analysis is on. The repair holds off while the whole directory looks lost (missing, unreadable, empty, or short of at least ten curves and more than a fifth of them), and stops after two failures of its own until the file or the bridge's build changes.

**A library root whose volume isn't mounted refuses writes, and is watched again once it mounts** (backlog B776, B777): an upload or a trash restore into it is refused (409 `root_not_mounted`) with nothing written, and the console's Try again resumes the same upload once the volume is back. A volume mounted over a root the bridge was already watching is watched again, checked every 30 seconds.

**The player checklist no longer points MPD at the bridge player's DAC** (backlog B1022): its DAC line (console and `bridge doctor`) told a reader to set MPD to the DAC the bridge's own player drives, which ALSA gives one player at a time. It now matches the two by ALSA card: the player's own DAC reads OK, and MPD set to the same card is a warning.

**A stop during serve's start ends it cleanly** (backlog B662): a stop that arrived while serve seeded its upscale target could end it with exit 1 and a database error (`sql: transaction has already been committed or rolled back`).

## Upgrade notes

**Nothing puts 0.2.3 back for you once 0.2.4 has run: the way back is by hand** (backlog B660, B1037): once 0.2.4 has opened its data directory, as its first start does once its settings load, the console's Roll back refuses, and so does the crash rollback, which puts the previous version back after three starts of the new one that did not end cleanly, once 0.2.4 has begun to migrate the database (schema 60 to 62), as that start does. To go back, stop the bridge, remove this release's new keys from `bridge.yaml` (the next two lines), switch `update.autoInstall` off if it is on, since 0.2.3 can install 0.2.4 again at a later check, and put the 0.2.3 binary in place of the bridge's: the updater's backup beside it, the binary's name with `.bak` added, until the start after 0.2.4's install is confirmed removes it, or else the 0.2.3 release's archive (with Docker, the `0.2.3` image on the same volume). Then start it: 0.2.3 opens and serves the migrated database as it stands, since it runs no migration step it lacks. A snapshot from before the update (`bridge restore`) is what puts the database itself back.

**Before going back to an earlier version, remove `tailscale.uploadDiagnosticLogs` from `bridge.yaml`** (backlog B749): a bridge from before this release refuses the key at load, whatever its value, and does not start; it also uploads Tailscale's diagnostic logs unless `TS_NO_LOGS_NO_SUPPORT=true` is in its environment.

**Before going back to an earlier version, remove this release's other new keys from `bridge.yaml`** (backlog B617, B761, B989): a bridge from before this release refuses `enrich.enabled`, `enrich.deezerEnabled`, `update.checkEnabled` and `adminLANAddress`, whatever their value, and does not start, and an update's automatic rollback is not made while one is there. Switching enrichment, the Deezer photos and the update check back on in Settings removes their keys; `adminLANAddress` takes removing by hand, and `tailscale.uploadDiagnosticLogs` has the line before this one.

**Reload any console tab left open across the update** (backlog B1029, B1037): a page loaded from 0.2.3 keeps running 0.2.3's script, and the Player page's no longer reads the player's status: where it shows the player's transports, it says "Couldn't read the player" at each refresh until it is reloaded.
