## The wire contract

- **A manifest response carries the history's epoch, a delta's `epoch=` that is not the bridge's answers `deltaIncomplete`, and a delta cursor is covered only below the stamp the next write would carry** (2026-11-17, backlog B653 PR 2b1). A restore of the library database alone (`bridge restore --database-only`) puts a snapshot's database in place of one whose history went on after the snapshot: the delta cursors the paired clients hold were issued by that discarded history, and its journal of deletions went with it. The restore's preparation mints a new epoch and restarts the deletion journal's coverage past the restored database's own stamps (`resetDeletionJournalCoverageTx`, unchanged), so a cursor issued before the restore reads as not covered wherever the discarded history's stamps never ran ahead of the restoring host's clock: such a cursor is below the restore's clock, which is at most the coverage start C, and C only moves forward. Where they did run ahead (the clock stepped back after the snapshot, or the history's stamps came from a host whose clock ran ahead), a cursor of the discarded history can sit at or past C and read as covered, and neither the snapshot's watermark nor the restore's clock knows it; the live database may not be opened to learn it. Two measures follow. **The clock guard**, server-side and for every client: `DeltaSinceCovered` answers covered only for `C <= since < next`, `next` the stamp the next write would carry (`selectNextDeltaStampSQL` at the store's clock: the later of the clock and one past `MAX(indexed_at)`, `MAX(deleted_at)` and the coverage start), the coverage and `next` read in one read transaction, so both describe one state of the store. Every cursor this history issued is below `next` (a response's `generatedAt` is the clock when it answered, a `library.changed` watermark is at most the stamp it names), unless the clock stepped back after it was issued, when a full sync is the safe answer anyway; so a cursor at or past `next` is answered `deltaIncomplete` until the clock or the watermark passes it. The strict `<` matters: `next` itself was issued by no write yet. **The epoch on the wire**, additive: every manifest response carries `epoch`, the backup epoch (the favorites' and the playlist list's), read once a response through `Store.BackupEpoch` (`responseEpoch`; none where it cannot be read, logged), and a delta may send back the epoch of the response its cursor came from (`epoch=`, read through `safeQuery` on the delta alone); one that is not the bridge's, or one sent where the bridge's cannot be read, answers `deltaIncomplete` with no `deleted` before the journal is asked, in `deltaDeletionFields`, which the built and the streamed legs share, so the two cannot drift. That decides every cursor a client sends with its epoch, whatever the clock did. An empty `epoch=` is sent and is not the bridge's, so it is incomplete too: a client that has a cursor and no epoch should send none, and an empty one is the conservative direction. The epoch is read once a response and never in the rows' transaction: inside a serving process it never changes, since a restore needs the store's lifetime lock exclusively and serve's open holds it shared. A paged full sync whose pages change epoch is the client's to restart (PROTOCOL.md). `/v1/health.features` says `manifestEpoch` on every bridge from this build on; it is not a setting. The B489 goldens drop `epoch` as they drop `generatedAt` (a store's epoch is random), and the streamed-and-built parity compares it. **Consequences.** A test that asks for a cursor ahead of a frozen store clock now reads it as not covered: `TestJournal_PruneAdvancesCoverage` asked for one a second past its frozen clock, and now moves the clock past it first, as every cursor a response issued is. The app's pairing verify sends `GET /v1/manifest?since=<future>`; that answers `deltaIncomplete: true` now, a 200 with no tracks as before, and the companion PR checks the verify step reads only the status and the decode. **Residuals**, by backlog id alone: an app that sends no epoch, on a history whose stamps ran ahead of the restoring host's clock, once the clock or the restored watermark has passed its cursor (B921); and what a cursor the clock has passed again leaves, for every client, with no restore at all (B919), whose scope `TestACursorPastAnythingThisHistoryIssuedIsIncomplete`'s last case pins as it stands. Rejected: rejecting a cursor above the current watermark (it holds only until writes pass the cursor, and refuses every `generatedAt` cursor of an idle library); taking an empty `epoch=` for none sent; reading the epoch in the rows' read transaction (nothing in a serving process changes it).
