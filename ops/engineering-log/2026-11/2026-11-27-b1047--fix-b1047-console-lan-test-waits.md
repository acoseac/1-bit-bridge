## 2026-11-27 — B1047: B989's console-LAN serve tests wait for the home-network socket and the state serve publishes

### Why

In four days four of B989's console-LAN serve tests (`cmd/bridge/console_lan_serve_test.go`) failed Windows CI on PRs that touch no console-LAN code, each a different test: `TestServeOpensTheConsoleOnItsLANAddress` with "no LAN listener" (B1000, run 45166022500, 2026-11-23); `TestServeHoldsItsLANSocketThroughConsolelan` with its state still `waiting`/`starting` (runs 45680079438 and 45992972490); `TestRemovingTheKeyLeavesTheVariablesListenerAndTheDoctorSaysSo` with "no LAN listener" at its `requireLANServes` (run 46003278454, attempt 1).

`startLANServe` returned once serve's banner named its API: `waitForListening` (`cmd/bridge/main_test.go` 197 to 232) looks every 25 ms (226) for the `https://` line and the fingerprint after "TLS fingerprint", which serve prints at `cmd/bridge/main.go` 7174 and 7213. Serve makes the home-network socket's first attempt after them, on its own goroutine (`BeginConsoleLAN`, 7264), and writes the home-network banner once that returns (7265 to 7267). A test that read the socket or the state at once could therefore read before the attempt or inside it: the stand-in box's client answers "no LAN listener" while the box has registered no listener (at e1f86c4a, `console_lan_serve_test.go` 120 to 124; the listen seam registers at 96 to 100), and `GET /api/console` answers the start's own `waiting`/`starting`, which `PrepareConsoleLAN` stores before the console's goroutine starts (`internal/admin/console_lan.go` 277 to 281). The loopback console can answer late as well: its goroutine starts before the banner (main.go 7102), but the handed listener only queues connections until `ServeListener` serves, and `lanServe.console` fails the test at a transport error. On this Mac the attempt had reached the listen seam by the time `startLANServe` returned in every red-first run below, so the window was the bind's last few statements; a loaded runner widened it. CI printed the failing state one line after "console listening on the home network", which fits a failure printed when its test ends, as `go test` prints one without `-v` (the Windows leg's run, `.github/workflows/gate.yml` 642), after the line the bind logged once it answered.

### The premise: the order is the product's contract

At e1f86c4a `consoleLAN.bind` (`internal/admin/console_lan.go` 511 to 587) publishes `listening` only after a successful bind with the policy installed. The listen (528) runs the hold's Control inside `ListenConfig.Listen`, and a failure returns there, with nothing stored. On success it builds the server and the fence that holds the policy (544 to 556), then, in one section of the plan's mutex, checks the stop (564 to 569), installs the policy the fence admits by (`current = p`, 570 to 572) and stores the state as listening (573); then it launches `http.Server.Serve` (576 to 583) and logs the listening line (584). A client that connects once the state reads listening waits on the bound socket until Serve accepts it, so no client of the product sees a state the socket does not keep, and the tests' requests on the LAN socket remain the proof that the console serves there. A failed attempt stores its waiting state under the mutex and says its WARN once it has let the mutex go (`setWaiting`, 351 to 367). An empty key's plan stores `off` in `PrepareConsoleLAN` itself (260 to 264), before the banner. No product change.

### The gate

The serve box's listen seam holds the bind, outside the box's mutex, until the test lets it go (`listenGate`): without `afterBind` inside `seams.listen` before the box records the stand-in, so the state still says starting and the box holds no listener (`box.count() == 0`), or with `afterBind` after the box records the stand-in and before the listener returns, so the box's order reads `hold,control,bind` while the state still says starting. Neither holds a listening state with no box listener. The bind's answer, the state and the banner wait for the release. `startLANServe` launches serve through `launchServe` with `runServe`, as `serveWithOpts` does, so that it can register the release in a cleanup that runs before the drain (`launchServe`'s `beforeBanner`), whatever the boot did; `lanServe.stop` lets the gate go before it waits for serve's exit. A hold never keeps serve from its exit. Both waits let a held bind go once a read made while it held has not found what they wait for: the bind then answers after the test has read the state it keeps back, with no timer.

`TestServeOpensTheConsoleOnItsLANAddress` runs its case three ways: as serve binds, with the bind held before its registration, and held once recorded. The two held rows are the waits' regression guard: a wait that takes the first answer that decodes (NC1 below) is red in both, and one that asks for the box's listener alone (NC7) in the row held once the bind is recorded alone, so that row guards the state's half of the listening wait. Neither row exercises a listening state with no box listener, so neither guards the half that asks for the box's listener (NC9).

#### What changed

- **One request, on a transport of its own.** `firstLANState` (`cmd/bridge/console_lan_serve_test.go` 450 to 540) sends its GET with `RoundTrip`, on one `http.Transport` it builds before its loop with `DisableKeepAlives` set, and defers that transport's `CloseIdleConnections` (489 to 496, 504). `RoundTrip` follows no redirect, so a 3xx is the answer; `Client.Do` follows one by default, and parses a `Location` before `CheckRedirect` runs, so even a client told to stop at the first answer lost the status of a 302 whose `Location` does not parse (`net/http/client.go` 655 to 665 and 711). A transport that keeps no connection has none to send the GET again on, which Go does after a read failure on a reused connection (`net/http/transport.go` 844 to 889). The request's one lifetime is `lanWaitContext`, which ends at the give-up and at serve's exit: the 5 s client timeout is gone. The loopback socket is bound before serve starts (`holdAdmin`), so a GET sent before the console serves waits in the socket's backlog to be answered; the timeout would have ended it and sent another, and the console's first answer would have gone to a connection the client had closed.
- **Only a dial is tried again.** The transport's `DialContext` wraps a dial's failure in `lanDialError` (441 to 448), and that alone is tried again, after `lanWaitPause` and within the same bounds. Every other failure ends the test at once, through `fail` (483 to 488), which asks `lanWaitOver` first, so serve's exit and the deadline are reported in `lanWaitOver`'s words, never as a raw `context canceled`: an error before the answer's headers, a status other than 200, the body's read, the decode. Its words for a failure of the request are "its request failed", not "failed once connected" (`ae693657`), since the wait's end can cancel a request before its connection is made.
- **The status before the body.** A status other than 200 closes the body unread and fails, naming `resp.Status` (513 to 516): a 503 whose headers were flushed and whose body is held open is never waited for. Only a 200's body is read, whole.
- **The key by its exact name.** The body is unmarshalled into `map[string]json.RawMessage`, `lan` is looked up exactly, a missing key or `null` fails, naming it, and then the state is decoded, a decode error failing (522 to 536). The struct decoder matched keys without regard to case, so `{"LAN":{"state":"off","fileKeyAtBoot":"empty"}}` passed the test's assertions. The envelope takes no `DisallowUnknownFields`.
- **The probes' context.** The fallback test's three probes (the `/stats` page, the Host `A:P` and the Origin `http://A:P`) run in a func invoked at once (778 to 806), which defers `cancel()` itself, so the context's watcher ends once they have run, on a `t.Fatal` among them too, where it ended at the test's end, after the tick and the last wait; a bare block would have left the defer at the test's scope. It has no red control: it changes how long a goroutine lives, which nothing the test asserts reads.
- The docblock says the above, and what is true of a listener bound before serve starts; its "which a console that has not begun to answer gives" is gone.

#### The stop's 5 s

The two 6.3 s runs the section "The first answer, the doctor's whole answer, …" names (the WARN at Info at `05ef196d`, no WARN at `16227159`) came from neither helper, and of the two causes the brief left open, serve's 5 s shutdown grace or `fetchAdminJSON`'s 5 s client timeout with `waitLANState`'s retry, it is the first, by way of the console's own shutdown. The fallback test at `ae693657`, its steps timed by a `t.Logf` the overlay added at each, was run in fresh processes from a race binary:

- In 10 runs, 2 took 6.28 and 6.30 s; in both, 5.049 s of it lay between the start of the deferred stop and the end of the drain, every step before the stop as in a fast run (the stop begun about 1.2 s in), and serve's stderr said `shutdown: background writers did not drain within grace` (`cmd/bridge/main.go` 4229 to 4234).
- In 12 runs with every goroutine written out as that grace ran out, 2 were slow, and the dumps held no writer's goroutine and no `bgWriters.Wait`'s (it had returned): what was left were an idle client connection of the default transport and a connection of the console's server waiting for a request line, its `Serve` loop ended.
- In 14 runs with the loopback console's connections recorded (when each was accepted, its last state, the requests it carried) and its `Shutdown` timed, 3 were slow, and in each the `Shutdown` took 5 s and answered `context deadline exceeded`, beside a second connection accepted 11 or 12 ms before it began, still new, having carried no request; a fast run's console had one connection, which carried 9 requests.
- In 20 runs with each dial of the default transport logged (when, the local port, the goroutine that asked), 1 was slow: its second connection was dialed by the test's goroutine between the end of the Host probe and the end of the Origin probe, and accepted 132 µs later. (A first form of that log, every goroutine written out at each dial, slowed the dials past the race: none of 16 runs was slow.)

The cause: the Host probe closes its 421 answer's body unread (`console_lan_serve_test.go` 797), on the default transport. Go 1.27.3's transport drains a body closed early and puts its connection back in the pool only after `Close` has returned (`net/http/transport.go` 2628 to 2637), so the Origin probe, sent at once, can find no idle connection and dial a second one, which it does not use: it takes the first back once that is drained. The second sits in the pool having sent nothing, and at serve's stop the console's `Shutdown` counts it as active until it is 5 s old (`net/http/server.go` 3332 to 3337), so it waits out its whole window (`consoleShutdownWindow`, `internal/admin/admin.go` 2447, taken at 2401) and answers its deadline. The console's goroutine is one of the background writers serve's stop joins (`cmd/bridge/main.go` 7101 to 7103), whose 5 s grace begins a moment after that window, in a defer that runs after `adminCancel`'s (4222 to 4235; 7086 and 7087), so serve says the line where the goroutine's last steps after its window outlast that moment: in both slow runs of the 10 and of the 12, in 2 of the 3 of the 14, and in the one of the 20. Of these 56 fresh runs 8 paid it, and in one process seven runs of twenty. It costs the test 5 s and fails nothing; it is left as it is (under "What stays open").
