### The hazard, and why the tooling changed

Without a row the ZH3 is an unknown DAC on iOS, which converts its DSD to PCM. A wizard run
submitted on 2026-11-19 (app 2.2 (17), iOS 27.3) reported the port as `Fosi Audio ZH3`
(USBAudio, not as headphones). The submission stays outside the repository and names its
submitter, whose credit is the iOS presentation entry's; nothing here names a person.

Since B730 PR 2 (2026-11-18) the app builds its registry from this catalog's export, and no row
had been added to the catalog first, so its tooling assumed every row came from the registry. The
lint asked every `ios` line to cite the row at an iOS commit (`ios: DACProfile.swift <id> at iOS
<commit>`), which no commit holds for a new row, and accepted any commit of the right form. The
export's parity test held the export to the frozen baseline's row count, its rows by place and its
known siblings exactly, so any addition failed it. The brief's review added the two changes:

1. `ad977bea`: an `ios` line may name the dated wizard submission a row added past the registry
   came from, `ios: wizard submission <date>`, the date a real one, then the text's end, `, ` or
   ` (` and free text (`reSubmitted`, `submitted`); beside it a DoP claim that cites the registry
   is refused (`fromSubmission`). The lint cannot tell a row of the baseline from a new one; the
   export's test, which reads the baseline, now requires a baseline row to name its own registry
   row and an added row its submission.
2. `dcfbc0f4`: the parity is a function over the baseline and a declaration written in the test
   by hand (`additions`, of type `declared`): rows in the export's own shape, in order after the
   baseline's, the names their units reported and the known siblings they bring, each name with
   both matchers' answers. It keeps every check of the baseline (its SHA-256, its 21 rows at their
   places, field by field, its generic names), takes past the baseline's rows the declared rows
   alone, field by field, holds the known siblings to the baseline's 26 and then the declared
   ones, and replays the old corpus, the declared names and the declared siblings over the whole
   export, the added rows included: a row added last in order still takes a corpus name no
   earlier row holds (`FIIO KA1`, whose live answer is no row), which a replay over the
   baseline's rows alone would hide. `TestAnAdditionIsHeldToItsDeclaration` holds the parity to
   it with a fixture row.

### The row, by the iOS procedure's section 6

no caveat, since iOS shows no USB identity. `fosiaudio.zh3`, `Fosi Audio ZH3`, maker `Fosi Audio`
(no Fosi row before it, so it starts the maker's group), order 22.

- Names: the port name iOS reported, `Fosi Audio ZH3`, verbatim as the legacy pattern, the port
  name and the token (14 characters; no other row's names, no generic name and no sibling holds
  it, and it holds none of theirs). No excluded token is needed.
- DSD over DoP, from the `dsd` results: DSD64 answered `clickThenTone`, heard; DSD128 and
  DSD256 held the lock proxy on 3 of 3 attempts each with the listening answered `notSure`,
  so neither heard nor failed, and unrecorded; DSD512 never attempted, and the PCM sweep asked
  its 1,411,200 Hz carrier and got 768,000 Hz, which step 7 reads as `carrierRefused` (as on the
  FiiO KA17 and the Onix Beta XI2), so `refusedBitRates` holds 22579200 with a dated `refused
  DSD512` clause. The legacy ceiling is DSD64, not the draft's DSD256, which an app build before
  B730 PR 1 took from the lock proxy alone. The brief's first reading, no carrier refused, was
  withdrawn by its review.
- The DoP claim's source is the listening, `wizard submission 2026-11-19 (app 2.2 (17), iOS
  27.3)`; its statement is the maker's (below).
- PCM: every rate from 44.1 to 768 kHz negotiated exactly, 1411.2 kHz renegotiated to 768 kHz,
  so `maxRateHz` 768000; the rejected rate lies above the ceiling and needs no note. `bitDepth`
  32: the submitter's 32 and the maker's USB figure agree.
- Volume `unknown`: the answers give the ZH3 a volume control of its own that the iOS slider
  does not move, the `dacOwnsGain` shape, while DoP held with the tone heard at iOS volume 0.3,
  below iOS maximum. The hedged note is the iOS presentation entry's.
- Every time null. The IO buffer was not granted at 768 kHz (1.3 ms); no settle rung was clean
  (a click at 150, 250, 400 and 700 ms, silence at 1000 ms); no cold-start rung was clean (a
  click at +0, +300, +600 and +1000 ms); the PCM entry was not tested. The transition cushion
  stays unset, as on every row but the four Chord ones, and the soft clicks heard across PCM
  44.1 kHz, DSD64, PCM 96 kHz and DSD64 are written in the row's `ios` line with the rest.

### The maker's figures, read 2026-11-19

Fosi Audio's own site, read on 2026-11-19: the store's ZH3 product page ("supports up to PCM
768kHz/32bit & DSD512") and the ZH3's page, whose General Specifications give the maximum
sampling rates by input, USB "PCM 32bit/768kHz, DSD512", optical and coaxial "PCM 24bit/192kHz".
Neither says DoP or native: DSD512 is a DSD figure that names no transport, so the row holds it
as `statedWithoutDoP {dsd, DSD512}`, credited by `stated DSD to DSD512: maker 2026-11-19`, which
the player never reads and the export gives as `dsdWithoutDoPStatement` up to `dsd512`, its
source the maker on that day. The page also offers UAC 1.0 and 2.0 modes (1.0 for consoles); the
row records no caveat for it, as no other iOS-only row does.

### The siblings, and what the ZH3's row would give each

The store's listing on 2026-11-19 holds sixteen products that take USB audio from a host. Each is
a known sibling now, named as the ZH3 names itself (`Fosi Audio <model>`), the brief's six first.
What each name actually reports on iOS is not known; the shape is the ZH3's. Every value of the
ZH3's row is unmeasured on all of them. The volume mode is the dangerous one: the row's `unknown`
comes with a note to keep iOS at maximum and use the DAC's own volume, and on a unit whose level
the iOS slider sets, maximum is full output into the headphones. The DS1, DS2 and DS3 pages state
volume controls of their own; nothing says what the iOS slider does on any of them. Beyond that,
by each page's USB figures:

| Sibling | Its page's USB figures | The ZH3 value also wrong for it |
