### The hazard, and why the tooling changed

Without a row the ZH3 is an unknown DAC on iOS, which converts its DSD to PCM. A wizard run
submitted on 2026-11-19 (app 2.2 (17), iOS 27.3) reported the port as `Fosi Audio ZH3`
(USBAudio, not as headphones). The submission stays outside the repository and names its
submitter, whose credit is the iOS presentation entry's; nothing here names a person.

Since B730 PR 2 (2026-11-18) the app builds its registry from this catalog's export, and no row
had been added to the catalog first, so its tooling assumed every row came from the registry. The
lint asked every `ios` line to cite the row at an iOS commit (`ios: DACProfile.swift <id> at iOS
<commit>`), which no commit holds for a new row, and accepted any commit of the right form. The
export's parity test held the export to the frozen baseline's row count, its rows by place and its
known siblings exactly, so any addition failed it. The brief's review added the two changes:

1. `ad977bea`: an `ios` line may name the dated wizard submission a row added past the registry
   came from, `ios: wizard submission <date>`, the date a real one, then the text's end, `, ` or
   ` (` and free text (`reSubmitted`, `submitted`); beside it a DoP claim that cites the registry
   is refused (`fromSubmission`). The lint cannot tell a row of the baseline from a new one; the
   export's test, which reads the baseline, now requires a baseline row to name its own registry
   row and an added row its submission.
2. `dcfbc0f4`: the parity is a function over the baseline and a declaration written in the test
   by hand (`additions`, of type `declared`): rows in the export's own shape, in order after the
   baseline's, the names their units reported and the known siblings they bring, each name with
   both matchers' answers. It keeps every check of the baseline (its SHA-256, its 21 rows at their
   places, field by field, its generic names), takes past the baseline's rows the declared rows
   alone, field by field, holds the known siblings to the baseline's 26 (its dump at iOS
   3145606a; the dump at iOS 6a1241eb that B1 read held 24) and then the 16 declared ones, 42 in
   the catalog, and replays the old corpus, the declared names and the declared siblings over the whole
   export, the added rows included: a row added last in order still takes a corpus name no
   earlier row holds (`FIIO KA1`, whose live answer is no row), which a replay over the
   baseline's rows alone would hide. `TestAnAdditionIsHeldToItsDeclaration` holds the parity to
   it with a fixture row.
