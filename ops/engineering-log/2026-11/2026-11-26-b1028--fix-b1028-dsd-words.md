## 2026-11-26 — B1028: the Player page and the example config say what the player does with DSD

### Why

Since the player's DSD cutover (B728 B7) the bridge's own player plays DSD by its effective policy. Two texts said otherwise at a23aab63. The console's Player page carried in its transports panel `DSD on this player comes in a later version.` (`internal/admin/templates/output.html` 103), which no script hid: a loopback bridge's page showed it whenever the panel was shown, once the status answered with a player available or a player block that does not read (`internal/admin/static/app.js` 7960 to 7962); a public bridge's page has no panel. And `config/bridge.yaml.example` said that dsdMode defaults off and that native, auto and dop play DSD only under owner approval, DoP only with allowDoP, while its copyable sample set `dsdMode: off`, `dsdQualification: ""` and `allowDoP: false`. Copied and uncommented, that `dsdMode: off` is migrated at the player's first start as the DSD state's default mode off (`internal/player/dsdstate/migrate.go` 174 to 178), which refuses DSD on every DAC with no choice of its own (`internal/player/policy/policy.go` 681 to 682).

### The page

The hint is gone; nothing else named its id. The help panel keeps what its sentence says of DSD, which the code backs: a browser plays no DSF or DFF (`internal/admin/player_audio.go` 127 to 128), and the bridge's player plays a rate natively, as DoP or converted to PCM, by its effective set (`policy.go` 619 to 683). The sentence no longer points at the transports, which sit below it and whose columns this change leaves as they are (What stays open). The page still offers no DSD control.

### The example

The player paragraph no longer calls the player in development. Each sentence it says of DSD, and the lines that back it:

- off by default, Linux only, never on a public bridge: `internal/config/player.go` 697 to 708.
- DSD is no setting of the file, and the player decides it for its DAC by its DSD state, `<dataDir>/player-dsd.json`: `config/player.go` 91 to 92, `dsdstate/state.go` 24, `cmd/bridge/serve_player.go` 515, `policy.go` 328 to 350.
- the state keeps each DAC's choice as the 1-bit app writes it, `PUT /v1/outputs/{id}/dsd`, for the DAC the player is bound to, while the player is stopped: `cmd/bridge/serve_player_output_dsd.go` 13 to 14, 37 to 55 (`dsdstate.ByApp`) and 65 to 69 (`playback_not_stopped`); no production code writes a choice by the console.
- a fresh install's state holds the default mode auto, which a DAC with no choice of its own takes: `dsdstate/store.go` 157 to 166, `dsdstate/state.go` 340 to 341 and 375 to 379.
- under auto each rate plays natively, as DoP or converted to PCM, or is refused, by the DAC's USB identity (a profile's match, a generic board's IDs), the DSD formats it offers, a listener's answers, the evidence of its DoP, its gain and the PCM it takes for a conversion: `policy.go` 620 to 648 and 693 to 695.
- dsdMode, allowDoP and dsdQualification are legacy keys, read only to migrate an older config: `config/player.go` 94 to 100; each start of bridge serve warns of each one set: `cmd/bridge/main.go` 4037 to 4042 and `config/player.go` 291 to 329; its player migrates them once into the state: `serve_player.go` 515 to 516 and `dsdstate/state.go` 317 to 320.
- the owner's qualification report still reads all three: `cmd/bridge/output_play.go` 245 to 250; a plain `bridge output play` migrates them into a state of its own (256 to 263).

The sample assigns none of the three; their names stay in the prose.
