### Item 1: a record the backend writes on a goroutine of its own

`refusedNotStopped` (`playback_dsd_choice_lease_test.go:49-63`) takes the state's writes (`:52`),
asks a choice's write, and requires the writes unchanged (`:57`). The write it asks wrote nothing: `writeChoice` returns at the configuration lease
(`playback/dsd_choice.go:289`), which `takeLease` refuses at once while a session's record stands
(`:599-604`, `playback_not_stopped`). The write counted was the native open's own driver_accepted:
once the sink's open passed its check after open, `openNativeOutput` records it
(`playback/native.go:253-258`, `noteDriverAccepted`), through `recordDSD`, on a goroutine the backend
spawns (`spawnWrite`, `playback/backend.go:743-754`), off the audio path.

`playingDSD` (`:97`) calls the synchronous `d.play()`. The engine's Play answers on the session's
Audible (`playBegan`, `engine/transport.go:505-521`), so the case is past Playing, which the owner
publishes before it calls Start (`startSession`, `:634-649`). A successful play does not wait for the
evidence write, which runs on its own goroutine: nothing orders it before the snapshot, so it could
land between the snapshot and the check, and the case took it for the refused write's.

The fix (`cf0122aa`): `playingDSD` waits until the state holds the open's driver_accepted, the
evidence for the format the open asked, as `TestDriverAcceptedComesFromARealOpen` waits
(`playback_dsd_policy_test.go:208`). The order the case relied on is now one its helper makes. The wait
cannot stall: `initialPolicy` holds no evidence (`playback_dsd_lifecycle_helpers_test.go:221-231`);
`playingDSD`'s six callers, three cases of `TestEachStateThePlayerIsNotStoppedInRefusesTheWrite` and
three of `TestEachStateThePlayerIsNotStoppedInRefusesTheTone`, each call it once on a rig of its own
and expect the record (a `cleanup` of `close` fails the sink's teardown, never its open); and a state
that held the evidence already answers the wait at once, `noteDriverAccepted` then writing nothing.

Forced order, `-run 'TestEachStateThePlayerIsNotStoppedInRefusesTheWrite/native_DSD_playing'
-count=20 -args -watchdog=5s`: the case builds its rig with the policy's store a `rigHeldStore`, armed
before `playingDSD`, so the driver_accepted write is held in `rigHeldStore.Update`, its release
registered once the rig is built, so it runs before the rig's stop; right after `refusedNotStopped`'s
snapshot the harness waits for the held write to come, lets it go and waits until it has landed.

### Item 2: a store case that could pass without its store

A round's budget: `ask` makes the round's context, `context.WithTimeout(ctx, timing.round)`, 300 ms
here (`serve_probation.go:506`), before the round's accept probe, its `/v1/health` and its store read,
in that order (`readiness.round`, `:340-351`), and waits for the round's answer `timing.round` and
`roundGrace` (`:486`, a second): the 1.3 s of CI's message (`:516`, `:523`). A check that takes the
cancel ends within the grace; `ServeHTTP` takes none.

What the handler's first `/v1/health` runs in this fixture: the store's two readiness probes
(`firstIndexedAtReady` and `sampleFormatReady`, `api/api.go:2132` and `:2253`, through `stickyReady`,
`api/health_counts.go:180-224`) and the roots' reachability probe (`probeAllRoots`, its stat at
`api/reachability.go:169`), each on `context.WithoutCancel` of the request's context with a 2 s bound
of its own, so the round's end does not end them. Its endpoint list is empty here: the fixture's
listen address names port 0, and `ReachableEndpoints` returns before its interface walk and its
Tailscale status read (`api/api.go:2397-2409`). The accept probe ends by the round's 300 ms and the
store read at the round's cancel, so an answer past 1.3 s is a `/v1/health` that took more than a
second, or a round left unscheduled for a second.

So the case could not tell two outcomes from the case it is named for: a `/v1/health` past 1.3 s failed
the round at its bound, CI's red; and one past 300 ms and under 1.3 s handed the store a context
already ended, so the read returned at once with `context.DeadlineExceeded`, the round said "the store
did not answer a read", and the case passed with no store waited on.

The fix (`b9fb5824`): `/v1/health` answers this version at once (`healthAnswersAtOnce`, through
`wrapAPIHandler`, as `TestAHealthAnswerOfAnotherVersionIsNotReady` stubs it), so the round reaches the
store with its 300 ms; the round keeps them (production's is 5 s, `serve_probation.go:75-76`). The store
(`unansweredStore`) records whether each read was entered with its round's context live and what ended
it, and names the read in its answer (`read #n`). The case judges the first round whose read was
entered live: not ready, the store named, the read ended by `context.DeadlineExceeded`, the round's
deadline. A round whose setup outran its context (a read entered with its context ended, a bound's
answer, a round refused beside one still out) is logged and passed over, neither failed nor taken
for the store's, so the case does not rely on 300 ms winning a race; a round whose read was entered
live is judged whenever it comes, and the wait for a round not yet come is bounded by the test
binary's deadline (`waitForCount`, through `serveGiveUpTime`), while a round already queued was
returned whatever the time. (Corrected in place on 2026-11-21 by bridge lows 14: this said the case
failed only where no round's read was entered live within `withinTheTest` (20 s). That 20 s bounded
only the passing-over: a round passed over after it failed the case at once, though the next round
could have been entered live, a red that scheduling decides. Bridge lows 14 removed it, so the case
passes such rounds over until the binary's deadline, which bounds the case once `waitForCount`
hands out no round past that deadline, rounds already queued included: bridge lows 14's round 2,
before which a loop that had fallen behind could take queued rounds past it.)

Forced, `-run '^TestAStoreThatDoesNotAnswerIsNotReady$' -count=5`: a sleep before the round's first
in-process `/v1/health` (the request whose `RemoteAddr` is `127.0.0.1:0`), standing in for a slow
first call, then the handler (the base: the API's own; the fix: `healthAnswersAtOnce`); the base's
test instrumented line for line to log its first read's liveness and the round's time left.
